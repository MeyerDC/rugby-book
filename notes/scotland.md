## Framing decisions (the chapter's spine)

**Thesis fit — "rugby has no class; institutions do."** Scotland is the *establishment-vs-Borders*
case. The same 1871 origin that gave England a class weapon gave Scotland two rugbies inside one
union:
- **The elite institutional game** — Edinburgh Academicals, the former-pupils ("FP") clubs, the
  private schools (Fettes, Loretto, Merchiston), the Edinburgh/West-of-Scotland university men. The
  SFU/SRU as the most *puritanically amateur* of all the unions — amateurism moralized into national
  character.
- **The Border counter** — Melrose, Hawick, Gala, Kelso, Jed-Forest, Selkirk: mill-town, artisan,
  working-class rugby. Home of **Sevens (Melrose, 1883)** and the **Border League (1901, world's
  oldest league competition)**. The working-class pocket *inside* the establishment game — the
  institutional divide made visible on a map. This is the chapter's engine (parallels Wales's
  coalfield and Argentina's Tucumán, but as a *region within* rather than the whole nation).

**Motifs to thread (SUMMARY.md §4):** the amateur ideology as class weapon (Scotland took it
furthest); cricket-club/school incubators; cross-refs to England Gen 2 (the Schism), Wales
(coalfield), Argentina/Tucumán (the working-class interior beating the metropolitan side).

**Runs Gen 0–7** (the 7-gen tier, like England/Wales/South Africa) — the professional-era collapse
to two clubs is a full generation of its own.

## 1. The Crombie brothers & Edinburgh Academical FC

- **Francis Crombie** and **Alexander Crombie** — brothers, pupils at **Durham School** (England),
  where the Rugby-School handling game was played; they carried it to **Edinburgh Academy** on
  returning north. **Francis Crombie** became the **first captain** of the Academical club.
  → Source: *Edinburgh Academical FC* club history (edinburghaccies.com "history"); *Scottish Rugby*
  official heritage pages (scottishrugby.org).
- **Edinburgh Academical Football Club** — the former-pupils' club — is generally dated **1857–58**
  and claims to be **the oldest football club (of either code) in Scotland** and one of the oldest
  rugby clubs in the world. The **1858** date is the one most club/heritage sources settle on for the
  formal club; **1857** appears as the date of the first organised football among the Academicals.
  → Source: Edinburgh Academical FC history; World Rugby Museum heritage notes.
  → **VERIFY 1857 vs 1858** — sources split; club's own history is the tie-breaker.
- Others involved: the club drew on **Edinburgh Academy** present- and former-pupils; early fixtures
  were Academy pupils v Academicals, and Academicals v Edinburgh University. (Named individuals beyond
  the Crombies are thin — VERIFY against the club history.)

## 2. First recorded inter-school / club matches

- **Merchiston Castle School v Edinburgh Academy** — first played **1858**; frequently cited as **the
  oldest continuously contested rugby-football fixture in the world** (school-history and World Rugby
  Museum claims).
  → Source: Merchiston Castle School history; Edinburgh Academy school history; World Rugby Museum.
  → **VERIFY the "oldest continuous fixture in the world" claim** — often repeated, provenance worth
  nailing down; also confirm exact first-match year (1858 usually given).
- **Royal High School of Edinburgh** — its former pupils (**Royal HSFP**) are among the early Edinburgh
  football-playing bodies and a founding-era union club (see §7). Specific dated early fixtures for RHS
  in this window are less firmly recorded — VERIFY.
- General shape: earliest organised matches are **Edinburgh academy/school-based**, inter-school and
  school-v-FP, from the late 1850s into the 1860s. Precise scorelines from this era largely not
  recorded.

## 3. The "Green Book" (1868)

- **1868** — a common code of laws was drawn up to let Edinburgh's schools/clubs play each other under
  one set of rules; bound with **green covers**, hence the **"Green Book."** Associated primarily with
  **Edinburgh Academy** and **Merchiston** (and other Edinburgh clubs). It is usually described as the
  **first codification of rugby-football laws in Scotland**, a local reconciliation of differing school
  rules (rooted in the Rugby-School game, not a verbatim copy of Rugby's own laws).
  → Source: Scottish rugby heritage histories; club histories; Richards, *A Game for Hooligans*.
  → **VERIFY**: (a) exact authorship/committee; (b) whether it reproduced "Laws of Football as played
  at Rugby School" or a distinct local code; (c) **whether any copy survives** (I cannot confirm a
  surviving copy — treat as unknown). This is a well-repeated but thinly-primary-sourced item.

## 4. Role of the English public schools & the code split

- The handling ("carrying") code reached Scotland chiefly through **Scots educated at English public
  schools** — **Rugby School** itself and **Durham School** (the Crombies) — plus masters who had
  played the Rugby game. Edinburgh's academies adopted and adapted it.
  → Source: Richards, *A Game for Hooligans* (Ch. on origins); Tony Collins, *A Social History of
  English Rugby Union* (context on the school diffusion of the Rugby code).
- **Contrast with the association/dribbling code**: Scotland's earliest organised football in the
  1850s–60s in the Edinburgh academies was the **handling** game; the **association** (dribbling) code
  organised separately and later (Queen's Park FC, 1867, Glasgow). So in this enclave the *handling*
  code has priority in the east/academy world. Good motif hook: same "English public-school export"
  mechanism as the informal-empire chapters, but here it lands *inside Britain*.
  → **VERIFY** Queen's Park 1867 only as context (not a rugby fact); don't lean on it.

## 5. West of Scotland FC & the Edinburgh–Glasgow spread

- **West of Scotland FC** (Glasgow) — founded **1865** (some sources **1865**, occasionally given as
  1866); Scotland's principal early **Glasgow** rugby club and a signatory/founding presence in the
  1871 challenge and the 1873 union.
  → Source: West of Scotland FC club history; Scottish Rugby heritage.
  → **VERIFY 1865 founding date** and the exact spelling of early club status.
- **Glasgow Academicals** likewise an early western FP club. The **Edinburgh–Glasgow divide** is
  present from the start: the game is a two-city establishment affair (academies + FP clubs +
  universities), **not yet** a Borders phenomenon.

## 6. The 1871 challenge and the first international

- **The challenge letter**: dated **December 1870** (commonly **8 December 1870**), published in
  **Bell's Life in London** and reproduced in **The Scotsman** (and the Glasgow press). Signed by the
  **captains of five Scottish clubs**, challenging England to a 20-a-side match under the rugby
  (handling) rules — a reaction to C.W. Alcock's **association-rules** "England v Scotland" games at
  the Oval (1870), which Scots rugby men deemed unrepresentative.
  → Source: World Rugby Museum; Scottish Rugby history; Richards, *A Game for Hooligans*.
- **The five clubs/signatories**: the captains of **Edinburgh Academicals, West of Scotland, Glasgow
  Academicals, Royal HSFP,** and **St Andrews University / Merchistonians** — named signatories usually
  include **F. J. Moncreiff** (Edinburgh Academicals) and **A. H. Robertson** (West of Scotland).
  → **VERIFY the exact five clubs and all five signatory names** — lists vary slightly across sources;
  this is a headline fact for the chapter, so pin it to World Rugby Museum / SRU.
- **The match**: **27 March 1871**, **Raeburn Place** (Edinburgh Academicals' cricket ground),
  Edinburgh. **20 a side**, two halves. Crowd **~4,000**. Scotland captain **F. J. Moncreiff**; England
  captain **Frederick Stokes**. Umpire/referee **Hely Hutchinson Almond**, headmaster of **Loretto**
  (a lovely class-texture detail — the private-school headmaster adjudicating; Almond's famous line
  about giving the doubtful decision "to the side that shouted loudest").
  → Source: World Rugby Museum "first international"; Scottish Rugby; Calcutta Cup histories.
- **Result**: **Scotland won by a goal and a try to a try.** **Angus Buchanan** credited with **the
  first try in international rugby** (the grounding was disputed); **William Cross** kicked the
  **conversion** ("goal") — and Cross also scored a try. England's try scored by **Reginald Birkett**.
  → Source: World Rugby Museum; Scottish Rugby; Richards.
  → **VERIFY** the precise sequence (Buchanan try → Cross conversion; second Scottish try; Birkett's
  England try) and Birkett's name against the museum record.

## 7. Founding of the Scottish Football Union (1873 → SRU 1924)

- **Scottish Football Union (SFU)** founded **1873** (meeting in early 1873; **3 March 1873** is the
  date usually given), formed by the leading Edinburgh/Glasgow FP, academy and university clubs.
  → Source: Scottish Rugby official history (scottishrugby.org); World Rugby.
  → **This corrects the Step-0 mis-slot at 1854–1870 — 1873 is firm-ish; confirm 3 March 1873.**
- **Founding clubs** (commonly listed **eight**): **Edinburgh Academicals, Glasgow Academicals, West
  of Scotland, Royal HSFP, Merchistonians, Edinburgh University, St Andrews University,** and
  **Glasgow University** (lists differ — Warriston / others sometimes appear).
  → **VERIFY the exact founding-club list** against SRU; do not present as settled until checked.
- **Renamed Scottish Rugby Union in 1924** (when the SFU dropped "Football" to disambiguate from
  soccer / align with "rugby").
  → Source: Scottish Rugby history. → **VERIFY 1924** (matches Gen 3 anchor).

## 8. Class / institutional texture (the chapter's thesis anchor)

- The founding cohort is **overwhelmingly former-pupil ("FP"), academy and university clubs** drawn
  from Edinburgh's (and Glasgow's) **professional/middle classes**: Edinburgh Academicals, Glasgow
  Academicals, Royal HSFP, Merchistonians, Edinburgh & St Andrews University men. The **private
  schools** — **Fettes, Loretto, Merchiston, Edinburgh Academy** — are the game's nurseries; Almond of
  Loretto refereeing the first international epitomises the class world.
  → Source: Richards, *A Game for Hooligans*; Collins (amateur-ideology context); club histories.
- **No Border-town rugby yet in Gen 0.** The Border clubs post-date this window — **Hawick (1873),
  Gala (1875), Melrose (1877), Kelso, Jed-Forest, Selkirk** all form *from the 1870s onward*, i.e. at
  the tail of / after the founding. So **Gen 0 is purely the Edinburgh (+ Glasgow) establishment
  enclave**; the working-class Border counter is a **Gen 2** arrival. This cleanly sets up the
  "two rugbies inside one union" engine.
  → **VERIFY Border club founding dates** (Hawick 1873, Gala 1875, Melrose 1877 are the usual dates)
  when writing Gen 2 — for Gen 0 the only needed claim is *absence*, which is safe.

## Generation 7: The Reboot (2020–2026) — fact digest (VERIFIED, July 2026 pass)

**Frame:** the two-club nation produces its **best-ever group of players** ("golden generation") and
beats everyone — especially England — yet **never wins a Championship** and keeps going out of World
Cups at the pool stage. The two-club ceiling, now visible not as collapse (Gen 6) but as a permanent
lid on a gifted side. Talent supply rebuilt partly by **heritage/residency recruitment** (the thin
two-club base can't produce enough) — a modern institutional adaptation of the thesis.

- **Gregor Townsend** — head coach since **2017**; Scotland's longest-serving; recurrent "under
  pressure" but retained through 2026. (Sources: FloRugby; ESPN.)
- **The Calcutta Cup dominance.** In the **nine** meetings 2018–2026, **England won only two (2020,
  2025)**; Scotland held the trophy the other **seven** ("seventh time in nine years," Feb 2026).
  Key wins: 2018 **25–13** (Murrayfield); **2021 England 6–11 Scotland at Twickenham** — first
  Scotland win there since **1983** (38-yr hoodoo), Duhan van der Merwe try on debut + 2 Finn Russell
  pens, played behind closed doors (COVID); **2023 29–23 at Twickenham** (Duhan's solo run from
  inside his own half = **World Rugby Men's Try of the Year**; 3 in a row, first since 1972); **2024
  30–21 Murrayfield** (Duhan **hat-trick** — first Scot to score 3 v England); **2025 England 16–15**
  ended four straight Scottish wins; **2026 Scotland 31–20** (7th in 9). (Sources: Sky Sports; ESPN;
  England Rugby; FloRugby.)
- **Golden generation — the players.** **Finn Russell** (Stirling → Glasgow → Racing 92 → Bath), the
  fly-half genius, "best playmaker on the planet"; **Duhan van der Merwe** (South-African-born wing,
  Scotland's **record try-scorer**); **Sione Tuipulotu** (Australian-born centre, qualifies via
  **Greenock grandmother Jacqueline Thomson**, named **captain Oct 2024**, scored v Australia Nov
  2024 27–13 Hopetoun Cup; **2025 Lions**, scored first try of the Test series in Brisbane); **Blair
  Kinghorn** (→ Toulouse, Champions Cup winner); **Huw Jones**; **Darcy Graham** (Hawick); **Stuart
  Hogg** (Hawick — a Borderer — retired 2023); **Pierre Schoeman, WP Nel** (SA-born props). Note:
  the few Borderers (Hogg, Graham) had to leave the Borders to be developed via city academies —
  the Gen 6 thesis payoff in person. (Sources: Wikipedia; Scotsman; Lions; Planet Rugby.)
- **Glasgow Warriors — URC champions 2024.** **21–16 v Vodacom Bulls at Loftus Versfeld, Pretoria**;
  came back from **13–0**; tries Scott Cummings, George Turner, Huw Jones; George Horne kicking;
  coach **Franco Smith**. **Second major pro trophy** by a Scottish side (after Glasgow's 2015
  Pro12) — again a **city**, not the Borders. First side outside SA/Ireland to reach a URC final.
  (Sources: Wikipedia 2024 URC Grand Final; Sky Sports; Glasgow Warriors.)
- **The ceiling — World Cups.** **Third straight pool-stage exit**: 2011, 2019, and **RWC 2023**
  (France), Pool B "pool of death" with **South Africa & Ireland** + Tonga & Romania. Beat Tonga
  **45–17** and Romania **84–0**; lost **South Africa 18–3** and **Ireland 36–14** → out. A golden
  generation that never reached a World Cup knockout. (Sources: Scotsman; autumn-internationals.)
- **The ceiling — Six Nations.** **Never won the Six Nations** (since Italy joined in 2000); best
  finish **third** (four times). **2026:** finished **third** again; on the final day **Ireland beat
  Scotland 43–21** — a **12th consecutive** Irish win over the Scots — denying a first title/Triple
  Crown in 36 years. France won the 2026 title. (Sources: ESPN Ireland 43–21 Scotland; Wikipedia
  2026 Six Nations; Rugby World.)
- **Structural contrast to thread:** Ireland (four provinces) became world No. 1 with Grand Slams;
  Scotland (two clubs) kept hitting the same lid. The two-club trap as a talent ceiling.
- **Mid-2026 snapshot (chapter cutoff):** Townsend still in charge; pressure eased by the 31–20
  Calcutta Cup win but a third-place 2026 and the Ireland defeat left the title drought at **27 years**
  (since 1999). Scots prominent on the **2025 Lions** tour of Australia. Question toward **RWC 2027 in
  Australia**: can a two-club nation with a golden generation finally break the ceiling — or is the lid
  structural?
