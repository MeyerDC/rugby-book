## THE SPINE (author decision, carried from SUMMARY.md §5A)

Australia is **the control case against New Zealand**. Same British colonial origin, the neighbouring
country, the same decade, the same rulebook posted out from England — and the opposite outcome.
New Zealand's game acquired no class character because the institutions that carried it (provincial
unions, country clubs, men who moved towns for work) had no interest in sorting people. Australia's
game acquired one because the institution that carried it **did**: the Great Public Schools of Sydney
and Brisbane. When rugby league arrived in 1907–08 it took the working class and kept it, and union
was left holding the schools.

**England's 1895 schism, re-run in the southern hemisphere twelve years later — with one difference.**
In England the union expelled the clubs. In Australia the players walked, and the union let them go,
because the schools were still there. `07-england.md` carries the George Hotel; this chapter carries
the other end of the same argument.

**The variable is visible for ten years, and this is the chapter's ⭐ set-piece (Gen 2).** In Queensland
the GPS schools themselves switched to league. The QRU **disbanded in 1919**. Union in Queensland did
not exist for a decade. It came back in **1928–29** when two schoolmasters — reportedly **Jack Ross**,
sportsmaster at **Nudgee College**, and **Canon Morris**, headmaster of **Churchie** — took the GPS
competition back to union, and within a year the QRU was reconstituted. *Same state, same players,
same climate; the schools left and the code died, the schools returned and it lived.* Not the founders
— the schools. This sits alongside **Tucumán** (Argentina), the **Borders** (Scotland) and
**Limerick v Cork** (Ireland) as the book's institutional controlled experiments.

## GENERATION MAP (provisional — Step 0, August 2026)

**BOTH CODES, BRAIDED — author decision, 23 Aug 2026.** League is not a Gen 1 event that union
survived; it is a parallel history running the length of the chapter, told from inside, exactly as
`07-england.md` runs the RFU and the Northern Union as two Englands. Australia needs this more than
England did: in England union remained the larger code, but **in Australia league won** — it is the
winter game of New South Wales and Queensland, the two states that *are* Australian rugby. A chapter
that follows only the Wallabies would be describing the smaller code and calling it the country, and
would break the thesis, which depends on seeing **what the working class went to**, not merely that
they left.

**And the refinement that makes Australia different from England — get this right, it is easy to
overstate.** The *rule* was identical in both countries: union's amateur bar made a crossing to league
permanent, and it held until 1995. What differed is **what the wall partitioned**. In England the
schism became a **geographic** partition — league in the northern industrial counties, union in the
south — so the two codes could spend a century barely touching. In Australia both codes stayed in the
**same two states, the same two cities, and largely the same schools**, so the wall ran between
classes rather than between regions, and it was crossed constantly by individual men rather than
respected by whole populations. League there was founded *by union players* out of the Sydney game;
Queensland's GPS schools carried league for a decade and then carried union again; and the traffic
never stopped — McLean playing league at school and captaining the Wallabies, McDermott going one way,
and after 1995 a long line coming back the other. **Australia did the sorting more completely than
England did, in one set of cities, with one set of schools.** That is the chapter's argument, and it
is why the two codes have to be told together.

### Gen 0 addendum — second pass, 23 Aug 2026 (verification + two architecture findings)

**⭐⭐⭐ THE RIVAL CODE WAS INVENTED BY A RUGBY SCHOOL OLD BOY, IN A CRICKET CLUB. (ADB, verbatim —
gold-standard, hand-retrieved.)** On **Thomas Wentworth Wills (1835–1880)**, cricketer and footballer,
born Molonglo Plains NSW: *"He was educated in Melbourne until 1852 when he went to **Rugby School**
where he **played football and captained the cricket XI**."* He returned to Melbourne in 1856 and was
**secretary of the Melbourne Cricket Club in 1857–58**. And: *"Wills's most famous letter was in
**Bell's Life in Victoria, 10 July 1858**, calling for **cricketers to take up a winter sport for
fitness' sake**. The response to this letter enabled him, his cousin **H. C. A. Harrison**, and others
to **draw up rules for a football game later to be known as Victorian or Australian Rules**."*

→ **THIS MAY BE THE CHAPTER'S REAL OPENING, AND IT CHANGES WHAT THE CHAPTER IS ABOUT.** Everywhere

### Gen 0 third pass — the institution, and a cold open (23 Aug 2026)

**⭐⭐⭐ THE BEST SINGLE FACT IN THE CHAPTER, AND THE INSTITUTION SAYS IT ABOUT ITSELF.**
From the **AAGPS's own official history** (`aagps.nsw.edu.au/about-us/history`, hand-retrieved):

> "**Sydney Boy's High School applied for membership in March 1894, but they were not admitted until
> 14 February, 1906.**"

**Nearly twelve years** (March 1894 to February 1906 — say "nearly", the arithmetic is 11 years
11 months). The Great Public Schools of New South Wales are not public schools — they are
private schools using the English term, exactly as `07-england.md` explains it. When an actual
*state* high school asked to join, it waited from **March 1894 to 14 February 1906**. No commentary is
required and none should be added: **print the two dates and move on.** This is the chapter's thesis
stated by the institution about itself, in its own words, on its own website, in 2026.

**✅ THE INSTITUTION, VERIFIED FROM ITS OWN RECORD:**
- Formed out of a meeting at **Gunsler's Café, near Circular Quay, on 30 March 1892**, "to consider the
  advisability of **concerted action by the Great Public Schools in drawing up regulations in athletic
  contests**" *(their quotation marks)*.
- **First meeting**: The King's School, Saint Ignatius' College, St Joseph's College, All Saints
  College Bathurst, North Shore Grammar School (SCEGS). **12 April**: Sydney Grammar School, Newington
  College, Cooerwull Academy. **Third meeting, 28 April 1892**: St Patrick's Goulburn, St Stanislaus'
  Bathurst, The Scots College.
- Composition since inauguration: "two Catholic, three Anglican, a Presbyterian, a United (formerly
  Methodist), one non-dominational and one non-religious non secular school"; **all but one took
  boarders**. *(Note the denominational spread — this is not one church's game, it is one class's.)*
- **Rugby: official competition from 1892**, called **"Football" until 2006**.
- **The First Grade Trophy was presented in 1896 by the President of the NSW Rugby Football Union**,
  engraved "School Challenge Trophy" — it replaced one won by **The King's School four years running,
  1892–95**. ⭐ *The union gave the schools their trophy: the governing body and the school system
  formally coupled, four years before Federation.*
- ⭐ "There was also a **Combined GPS team who annually played against a visiting English team from
  1899**" — the schoolboys got their own fixture against the tourists, in the year of the first Tests.
- **The AAGPS was founded in 1892 — the same year the Southern Rugby Union became the NSWRU.** The
  schools and the union organised themselves into their modern forms in the same twelve months.
- Institutional histories worth chasing if the chapter needs more: Robert A. I. Grant, *"Games to
  Play Out…" The Formation and Development of the AAGPS 1892–2017* (2017); J. Carington Pope, *Unity
  in Diversity* (1961); **Geoffrey Sherington**, *Unity and Diversity … since the Second World War*
  (1996) — Sherington is an academic historian of Australian education.

### Gen 1 — Messenger's Wage (1900–1914) — brief, 23 Aug 2026. ALL ADB unless marked.

**⭐⭐⭐ THE PAIRING THAT IS THE WHOLE CHAPTER, AND IT IS NOT "RICH v POOR".**
- **Herbert Henry "Dally" Messenger (1883–1959)** — b. 12 Apr 1883 Balmain, third son of **Charles Amos
  Messenger, boatbuilder**; nicknamed after W. B. Dalley; **Double Bay Public School**; then the family
  boatshed, *"in the **down-at-heel fishing and ferrying enclave within the otherwise upper-class
  harbour-side eastern suburbs**"*. Union with **Warrigal** from about 1900, **Eastern Suburbs** 1905.
  *"Just bung the ball out to me, boys, as quickly as you can."* 12 st (76 kg), 5 ft 7½ (172 cm).
- **Herbert Michael Moran (1885–1945)** — b. 26 Apr 1885 Darlington, second son of **Michael Moran,
  Irish-born baker**; **Darlington Public School**, then **St Aloysius' College**, briefly
  **St Joseph's College, Hunters Hill**, then the **University of Sydney (M.B. 1907)**. Rose Bay, then
  the university club; **captained the first Wallaby tour of 1908**; F.R.C.S. Edinburgh 1909; a
  distinguished cancer surgeon and a pioneer of radium therapy.
→ **Both were working men's sons. Both went to a public school.** The baker's boy got the education
  ladder — Catholic college, AAGPS school, university, medicine — and captained the amateurs. The
  boatbuilder's boy did not, and took the money. **Do not write this as class. Write it as which
  institution caught which boy.** That is the book's thesis at its sharpest.

**⭐ THE WAGE — the generation's title fact, with figures.** In **August 1907** Messenger *"played three
games against a New Zealand professional team, **receiving £180**"* — the All Golds passing through
Sydney (`12-new-zealand.md` Gen 2 has them from the other side). He was **"expelled from the Rugby
Union code"**, joined the New Zealanders for Britain, was **the outstanding player of the tour**, and
**returned to Sydney in April 1908 "with £200 in his pocket and well fitted out with new clothes."**
Then captained Eastern Suburbs to runners-up in **the inaugural Sydney Rugby League competition**, and
in August left again with **the first Kangaroos**, leading them in two Tests.

**⭐⭐ THE EXPULSION OF FOURTEEN — the hinge, and better than "players drifted to league".**
From **Chris McKivat's** entry: *"On returning to Sydney in **September 1909**, **fourteen of the
Wallabies played a series against the Rugby League Kangaroos**. McKivat, **allegedly paid £150**, was
**expelled with the others from the amateur code**."* And from **Sir James Joynton Smith's**: *"In
**August 1909** Smith **financed football matches between the (Rugby Union) Wallaby team and the
(Rugby League) Kangaroos**; proceeds went to Smith's favourite charity, **(Royal) South Sydney
Hospital**."*
→ The union's own Olympic champions played a **charity** series bankrolled by a hotel-keeper, and
  **fourteen of them were expelled for it.** ⚠️ Supersedes rugbyfootballhistory's "eleven of its
  members were enticed" — use **fourteen**, and use the mechanism, which is the 1909 series.

**⭐ McKIVAT — and the fact that breaks any lazy "union = private schools" sentence.**
**Christopher Hobart McKivat (1879–1941)**, fifth surviving of ten children of **Edward McKivat,
Irish-born farmer**; by 1897 playing senior union for **Bowen Bros Tannery's team at Orange**;
selected to tour NZ in 1901 but **had to decline**; to Sydney 1905 and **Glebe** (the "Dirty Reds").
Chosen for the 1908 tour, he *"was presented by fellow workers at the **Farmers' & Dairymen's Milk Co.
Ltd** with an **inscribed gold watch**"*. Eight tries on tour; **led the Wallabies seventeen times**
while Moran and vice-captain Freddy Woods were injured — *"including the match against **Cornwall** at
the London Olympic Games when the Australians won gold medals."* Later led the second Kangaroos
1911–12, playing 32 of 36 matches. Labourer, engine driver, storeman, weigh-clerk; married a
tailoress; buried at Botany, as Messenger was. *"Reputedly Australia's finest half-back in either
Rugby code."*
→ **A tannery team in Orange, a milk company's workmates clubbing together for a gold watch, and the
  man captains an Olympic gold medal side.** Country and works rugby existed and mattered — the
  chapter must not flatten Australian union into Sydney's GPS.

**⭐ THE 1908 OLYMPIC GOLD, AND THE JERSEY.** Australian Olympic Committee, hand-retrieved:
"**15 members** of the Australian rugby union team arrived in London"; the team was "**nicknamed the
Wallabies by the English Press**"; England and Australia were the only entrants, so a **one-off game
against Cornwall, the English champions**, decided it; **Australia won 32–3**. And ⭐⭐: *"The
Australian team … **did not play in the now-traditional green-and-gold colours, but wore light-blue
jerseys with a waratah on the left breast**."*
→ **CALL BACK TO GEN 0'S CLOSING LINE.** In 1899 there was no Australia and the side wore the host
  colony's colours. In **1908**, seven years after Federation, Australia won an **Olympic gold medal
  in New South Wales light blue**. The nation existed; the jersey had not caught up.
- **Daniel "Danny" Carroll**, 20, the "baby" of the side; born Melbourne, educated Sydney, played for
  **St George while studying dentistry**; **15 tries** in the British Isles, **two against Cornwall**;
  toured North America in 1912 and **stayed**; a **US Army lieutenant** in the war; **playing coach of
  the United States side that won Olympic gold at Antwerp in 1920** and non-playing coach of the 1924
  winners — **gold medallist for two countries, the only Australian to manage it.**
- **Reginald "Snowy" Baker** took **silver in middleweight boxing** at the same Games.

**⭐⭐ THE OTHER CODE BUILT AN INSTITUTION, AND IT WAS THE LABOR PARTY'S.**
- **Edward Rennix "Ted" Larkin (1880–1915)** — b. North Lambton, Newcastle, third child of
  **William Joseph Larkin, quarryman and miner**; **St Benedict's, Chippendale** and **St Joseph's
  College, Hunters Hill**; a journalist. Played union for Newtown, captain 1903, and **for Australia

  Brothers' schools to play Rugby League in 1913."** *"Under his guidance the code came to be the

- **Fred Flowers (1864–1928)** — a **house-painter and plasterer**, son of a Staffordshire gardener;

**⭐⭐⭐ THE BEST SINGLE SENTENCE IN THE CHAPTER SO FAR — ADB on Lawton:**
> "**The Rugby Union suspended Lawton as a suspected professional in 1923 on a charge of having played
> Rugby League in Queensland, but he was exonerated when it was shown that there was no Rugby Union
> available at the time.**"

A Queenslander charged with professionalism for playing league — and acquitted because **union did not
exist in his state**. The amateur bar prosecuting a man for playing the only football there was.

**⚠️ CORRECT THE ARGUMENT — the sequence is not what Step 0 assumed.** Going *down*: the QRU collapses

reborn until the schools came back. **The honest claim is not "the schools killed it" — it is that the
union could die without the schools and could not be resurrected without them.** Write that, not the
neater version.

### Gen 3 — The Union Forms (1933–1961) — brief, 23 Aug 2026. ADB unless marked.

**⭐⭐⭐ THE 1939 WALLABIES PLAYED ONE MATCH, IN BOMBAY, SO THEY COULD WEAR THE JERSEY ONCE.**
ADB on **Bill McLean**: selected for the 1939 tour of Britain — "**The team played no games, however,
because of the outbreak of World War II on 3 September, the day after their ship berthed at Plymouth.
Instead, the touring party helped with war preparations by filling sandbags at Torquay. On 15 September
they were presented to King George VI and Queen Elizabeth, immediately before embarking for Australia.
The voyage was perilous, with the ship zigzagging to avoid enemy submarines. A match was hastily
arranged at Bombay (Mumbai), India, so the team could at least wear their Australian jerseys on the
field of play; the Wallabies won 21–0.**"

**⭐ BILL McLEAN (1918–1996) IS THE GENERATION'S SPINE, AND HE IS A TWO-CODE QUEENSLAND CHILD.**
Son of **Doug McLean, refreshment room manager**, himself "a pioneering member (1904–05) of the
national rugby union team". **Norman Park State School (1925–32), Brisbane State High (1933),
St Laurence's College (1934)** — *i.e. he was a Brisbane schoolboy in exactly the years the GPS were
switching back to union*. He **played rugby league for Queensland State Schools against NSW in
1931–32** and junior union in 1933–34. Engineering draughtsman; the **YMCA team in the QRU (1935–37)**,
then the **GPS Old Boys' club**. A wartime **commando** — Trevor Allan: "in uniform or not, he still
looked every inch a commando". Captained the 1946 Wallabies in New Zealand (three Tests, all lost) and
the **1947–48 tour** of Britain, Ireland, France and North America, where with the manager **Arnold
Tancred** he ran "non-stop ninety-minute sessions" using "the then novel **scrum machine**".
⭐ **"The Wallabies would win three of the five Tests on the tour, but McLean suffered a broken leg on
2 October 1947, which prevented him from playing in any of them."** At Twickenham against Combined
Services, hit by three tacklers — a spiral fracture of tibia and fibula, and "some spectators at the
top of the stadium claimed they heard the bones snap". He refused to leave the field:
⭐ **"I'm the captain of Australia. I'm staying! If somebody runs by, I'll get him."**
Afterwards a newsagent, publican, milk vendor and van salesman who taught disabled children to paint.
**By 1978 the McLean clan had produced five more internationals** — brothers Alexander and Reginald,
son Peter, nephews Jeffrey and Paul. *(Arnold Tancred is the brother of Harry Tancred from Gen 2's
notes — he managed the 1947–48 tour and was NSWRU president in 1959.)*

**✅ THE NATIONAL BODY, 1949 — NOW ADB-SOURCED.** From **Tom Blue's** entry: he was "a Queensland
delegate to the **Australian Rugby Football Union (1949–52)**, and **ARFU deputy chairman (1949,
1951)**." → The ARFU's existence and its 1949 start are confirmed by a scholarly source; the fuller
detail below is **NOT** (australia.rugby is bot-blocked — attribute, do not assert):
⚠️ *reported only*: the state unions agreed in **1947** to form one administrative body; "added impetus
came in **1948** when the International Board invited **Australia specifically (rather than a New South
Wales representative)** to take a seat"; the constitution ratified **25 November 1949** by **eleven
delegates** from NSW, Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia, Tasmania and Victoria; ACT
admitted 1972, Northern Territory 1978.
→ **If the IRB-seat detail can ever be verified it is the best fact in the generation:** Australia
  built a national governing body because somebody offered it a chair and there was nobody to sit in it.

**⭐ AND UNION WAS NOT ONLY THE GPS — Tom Blue proves it twice.** An **Eastern Suburbs hooker** and
first-grade referee for over a quarter of a century who, moved to **Dubbo in 1932 as a general line
salesman for Goodyear**, "together with a local rugby international, **Bryan Palmer**, helped to form

### Gen 6 — Two Wars in One Year (1992–2003) — brief, 23 Aug 2026

**⭐⭐⭐ THE SUPER LEAGUE WAR, FROM THE LAW REPORTS.** Source: **australiancompetitionlaw.org**, which
reproduces the judgments with citations — far better than the fan histories, and hand-retrieved.
- The ARL and NSWRL got the clubs to sign **"Commitment Agreements"** — executed in "substantially
  identical form … within a short time of each other", around a meeting of **14 November 1994** —
  binding them to the NSWRL competition for **five years**; then **"Loyalty Agreements"** after a
  meeting of **6 February 1995**, at which ⭐⭐ "**a motion was passed requiring all clubs to sign the
  Loyalty Agreement on pain of expulsion**."
- The appeal court found the clubs "could hardly fail to understand" they were "being asked to join
  all other clubs in undertaking **not to participate in an unauthorised competition**"; there were
  references at the meeting to the need for clubs to "**stick together**".
- **Justice Burchett** rejected News's case at trial — *News Ltd v Australian Rugby Football League Ltd*
  (1996) 58 FCR 447, **23 February 1996**. The **Full Federal Court** reversed him, holding the
  Commitment and Loyalty Agreements were **exclusionary provisions contravening s 45(2)(a)(i)** of the
  Trade Practices Act and **void** — severance impossible because the exclusionary terms were
  indivisible. **Leave to appeal to the High Court was refused.**
- **1997: two competitions ran side by side.** **1998: the NRL**, a joint venture of the ARL and News.

**⭐⭐⭐ AND THEN THEY THREW OUT SOUTH SYDNEY — the club of Fred Flowers (Gen 1) and Clive Churchill
(Gen 3), "pride of the League", "embedded in a red-blooded working-class district, based on Redfern".**
Part of the ARL–News deal was cutting the competition **from 17 teams to 14**, by ranking clubs for
"suitability". ⭐ **"In this ranking, the South Sydney Football Club was ranked 15th and was therefore
excluded from the competition."** Ranked fifteenth for fourteen places.
The litigation, all citable:
- Interlocutory injunction dismissed by **Justice Hely** — [1999] FCA 1710, **9 December 1999**.
- **Justice Finn** rejected the claim — [2000] FCA 1541, **3 November 2000**.
- The **Full Federal Court** (Moore and Merkel JJ, **Heerey J dissenting**) **overturned Finn**, holding
  the 14-team term WAS an exclusionary provision — [2001] FCA 862, **6 July 2001**.
- The **High Court** reversed that, **4–1, Kirby J dissenting** — *News Ltd v South Sydney District
  Rugby League Football Club Ltd* **[2003] HCA 45**. Majority: "purpose" in s 4D is **subjective**, and
  News and the ARL had no subjective purpose of excluding any particular club. **Gleeson CJ**:
  "exclusivity is a necessary feature"; "the purpose of the 14 team term was to define the size of the
  competition".
- ⭐⭐⭐ **KIRBY J, DISSENTING — the quote for this generation:** "*As is usually the case in such
  matters, the restricting competitors assert that the best interests of the market or game or
  participants happen conveniently to coincide with their own best economic interests.*" (para 149)
→ **The case was decided in 2003 — the year of Australia's home World Cup.** Bookend the generation.

**UNION'S HALF — mostly already in the book, cross-reference rather than repeat.**
`12-new-zealand.md` Gen 6 carries the whole 1995 architecture: Murdoch's **Super League** against
Packer's ARL rights; Peter FitzSimons's "signed them up in **carparks at midnight**"; Packer's counter
through the Sydney lawyer **Geoff Levy**, **four million dollars** of seed money, the former Wallaby

**What is Australian and belongs here:** both proprietors were Australian, both wars were fought in
