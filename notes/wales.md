## GEN 0 BRIEF (1603–1880) — no-wiki; haiku retrieval adjudicated (July 2026)

**Sourcing reality (flag for all Gen 0):** pre-1880 Welsh rugby has few persistent citable web sources.
The spine rests on two books — **Dai Smith & Gareth Williams, *Fields of Praise* (WRU official history
1881–1981)** and **Gareth Williams, *1905 and All That*** — plus WRU/club/museum pages. In the prose,
carry facts on those named books; hedge anything web-thin. Do NOT overstate the archive as if verified
online.

### Cnapan
- **George Owen of Henllys, *Description of Pembrokeshire* (1603)** records cnapan as popular since
  "great antiquity." Manuscript at the **National Library of Wales**; the 1603 attribution is the
  standard one but the exact wording rests on the print edition, not a live web text — attribute to
  Owen, don't fake a verified quote page.
- Played in **west Wales** — Pembrokeshire, Carmarthenshire, Ceredigion. (Smith & Williams; G. Williams.)
- **Suppression as "riotous assemblies" / read as Welsh cultural resistance to Tudor assimilation is
  INTERPRETIVE**, not a documented edict. Frame with a named historian ("Smith and Williams read…")
  and hedge; do NOT state a suppression order as fact.
- Continuity to codified rugby is **metaphor, not genealogy** — cnapan faded; the "ghost wearing a
  jersey" is authorial framing. Keep it clearly figurative.

### Lampeter / Rowland Williams
- **Rev. Rowland Williams** appointed Vice-Principal & Professor of Theology, **St David's College,
  Lampeter, 1850**; **King's College, Cambridge** background. (Institutional history; documented.)
- **Arthur Pell** tried to introduce Rugby School rules at **Cambridge, 1839**; the Pell→Williams
  "ten-year memory" chain is **attested in rugby historiography, not archived witness** — hedge as
  transmitted account.
- **Prescott caveat (chapter-critical):** historians such as Prescott question whether Williams
  "introduced" rugby in any codified sense; better that he **built the sporting environment** (cricket,
  fives, rugby as Muscular Christianity) in which the game condensed. VERIFY exact Prescott work/page
  before quoting; if not, write "historians such as Prescott have questioned…" without a page cite.
- **Lampeter's claim to be the oldest rugby "club" (c.1850) is contested** — no match records before
  1866; Neath (1871) is the first properly constituted club. Frame Lampeter as institutional claim.

### First matches
- **1866: Lampeter College v Llandovery College — first *recorded* rugby match in Wales; score
  unknown.** Llandovery College founded 1848. (WRU; History Points memorial; club history.)
- **1856 inter-school claim = unverified tradition** — say 1866 is the first *recorded* match; hedge any
  earlier.

### First clubs (1871–1876)
- **Neath RFC 1871** — "widely recognised as the first Welsh club." Captain **T.P. Whittington capped
  for Scotland (1873)** — attested; verify via SRU/Almanack if quoting as fact.
- **Swansea & Llanelli 1872**; **first recorded club match Swansea v Neath, 7 Feb 1872** (canonical
  date; verify in club records).
- **Brecon 1874, Newport 1874, Mountain Ash 1875, Cardiff & Merthyr 1876** (Cardiff a merger of
  Glamorgan/Tredegarville/Wanderers); **Pontypool origins ~1868** (approximate).
- **Geography = the whole point:** graduates settled in the **coal and steel towns** (Neath, Merthyr,
  Mountain Ash, Pontypool) where the work was — not gentlemen's suburbs. This is the accident that
  differentiates Wales; documented in industrial/social histories and Smith & Williams.

### South Wales Football Club / Union (1875–1878)
- **South Wales Football Club founded Brecon, September 1875**, to play the principal clubs of the
  **West of England**, explicitly adopting the **Rugby code** (a written choice of code).
- **First representative fixture: SWFC v a Hereford XV, Castle Green, Hereford, December 1875** — Wales
  in **black and white**, won by a single try (**A.H. Richardson**, Swansea). First Welsh rep colours
  were NOT red. (WRU history.)
- **1878:** SWFC → **South Wales Football Union**; ran the **South Wales Challenge Cup** — a cup that,
  like the Yorkshire Cup, made winning matter and pulled the best local players in regardless of class.

### 1880–1881 — the union's birth
- **Tenby Hotel, Swansea, March 1880** — clubs meet, sketch a bigger structure. (WRU history; verify
  venue detail — some accounts; hedge lightly.)
- **19 Feb 1881, Blackheath:** first Wales v England international, arranged by Newport's **Richard
  Mullock** without official mandate; Wales lost **7 goals, 1 drop goal, 6 tries to nil ≈ 82–0** modern.
  **Two selected players didn't turn up**; side largely Oxbridge, savaged locally as unrepresentative.
- **12 March 1881, Castle Hotel, Neath:** **Welsh Rugby Union founded**, eleven clubs. Mullock became
  honorary secretary/treasurer (to 1892). **Do NOT assert the first president's name or the full
  eleven-club list as hard fact** — the detailed club roll and "Cyril Chambers, first president" trace
  only to heritage blogs (Made in Cymru) in this pass; hedge or omit. **Neath reportedly absent** from
  its own town's founding meeting — unexplained; treat as a noted oddity, one theory (self-interest via
  the South Wales FU), not documented fact; WRU only acknowledged Neath's likely role at its 125th
  anniversary (2005/06).

## GEN 1 BRIEF (1881–1899) — no-wiki; haiku web-verification adjudicated (July 2026)

### The coalfield / class-crossing (the thesis — attribute to Smith & Williams)
- Central Welsh argument: the South Wales coal boom of the 1880s–90s planted rugby in the pit and
  tinplate villages, and it became a genuinely **working- and cross-class** game — unlike England,
  where the same amateur code excluded working men. Carry this on **Smith & Williams, *Fields of
  Praise*** by name (the coalfield-employment thesis is theirs); don't assert it flat. Crowd/club
  growth figures are web-thin — describe growth qualitatively, hedge any hard number.

### 1893 Wales 12–11 England, Cardiff Arms Park — DOCUMENTED (Cardiff Rugby Museum)
- **7 January 1893.** Severe frost; ground "hard as a brickbat"; staff used ~**500 "fire-devils"**
  (coal-filled buckets, holes punched in them, on bricks) for ~**27 hours** to thaw the pitch.
- Score **12–11 to Wales**; confusing at the time because the **scoring values had just changed**.
  England's Howard Marshall scored three tries and still lost — do NOT reproduce a full point-by-point
  breakdown (scoring-rule flux makes it error-prone); keep to 12–11 + Marshall's three tries.
- **Billy Bancroft's kick decided it** — the famous story is he took the winning **dropped goal from a
  mark against his captain Arthur Gould's wishes** (Gould wanted a placed kick). Render hedged
  ("as the story is told") — exact mechanics vary in sources.
- This match **opened** Wales's first Triple Crown.

### First Triple Crown, 1893 — DOCUMENTED
- Wales beat **England** (12–11, 7 Jan, Cardiff), **Scotland** (5 Feb, Raeburn Place — score varies in
  sources, ~9–0; HEDGE the Scotland score), **Ireland** (3–0, Stradey Park, Llanelli). **Arthur Gould**
  captain. **Bert Gould** (Arthur's brother) scored the try that clinched it v Ireland.
- Wales's **first Triple Crown and its only one of the 19th century.**

### Four three-quarter system — DOCUMENTED (Cardiff Rugby official) — belongs in GEN 1, not Gen 2
- **Frank Hancock**, Cardiff RFC (Somerset-born, joined 1883–84). Cardiff first used the extra
  three-quarter (four threes / seven backs) in **1884–85** to fit Hancock in alongside centre Tom
  Williams; **1885–86** Hancock captained Cardiff to a near-perfect season (131 tries scored, 4
  conceded).
- Hancock captained **Wales v Scotland, 9 Jan 1886** in a first international attempt at the system —
  it failed that day and was scrapped mid-game; adopted nationally afterwards, used by the 1888 side v
  the NZ Natives, and fully embedded by the **1893** Triple Crown.
- **CORRECTION for the rewrite:** the old chapter implied the four-three-quarter was invented in the
  1900s (Gen 2). It is a **mid-1880s Cardiff innovation** — place it here.

### The Arthur Gould Affair (1896–1898) — DOCUMENTED (Sporting Landmarks; World Rugby Museum)
- Public testimonial for **Arthur Gould** (Newport, Wales captain, three-quarter, the game's first
  superstar), initially endorsed by the WRU. **~£700** raised; it bought/settled a house at **6
  Llanthewy Road, Clytha Park, Newport**, later known as **"Thornbury"**; the **title deeds were
  presented to Gould on Easter Monday 1897** at a Drill Hall dinner by WRU president **Sir J.T.D.
  Llewelyn**.
- The **International Rugby Football Board judged the gift a breach of amateurism** (effective
  professionalism) and threatened Wales's fixtures. **Scotland and Ireland refused to play Wales**
  (1897; Scotland again 1898, not resuming until **1899**); the **WRU briefly resigned from the IRFB
  (Feb 1897)**; a compromise came by **August 1897** after the RFU shifted position.
- Resolution: **Gould retired from international rugby** (last cap Feb 1897; retirement announced Jan
  1898). He lived at Thornbury until his death, **2 January 1919**.
- Framing (no-villain): the English/Scottish/Irish officials enforcing amateurism believed they were
  defending the game's founding principle; the Welsh public saw a nation honouring its greatest player.
  Both true. Cross-ref England/Scotland amateur-ideology motif.

### Going north — DOCUMENTED (Tony Collins; Swansea RFC) — with one figure dropped
- **David & Evan James** (Swansea half-backs, "the curly-haired marmosets") turned professional and
  went to **Broughton Rangers in 1892 — BEFORE the 1895 split** (good, accurate point: the northward
  pull predated the formal breakaway). Suspended **29 March 1893**; reinstated **Feb 1896**; returned to
  Swansea and played for Wales v England **1899**.
- After the **1895** Northern Union breakaway (George Hotel, Huddersfield, 29 Aug 1895 — see England
  chapter), recruitment of Welsh working-class players north became systematic. Source spine: **Tony
  Collins, *Rugby's Great Split*** and his *Rugby Reloaded* essay/podcast "Going North"; **Robert Gate,
  *Gone North*** counts **148 Welsh union internationals who switched to league 1895–1986.**
- **DROP the "six shillings a match" figure** — COULDN'T-VERIFY for this period (it was in the old
  chapter as a generic number). Describe the inducement generally (jobs + pay + broken-time) without a
  bogus precise figure.

### Early internationals — DOCUMENTED
- **First win over England: 15 Feb 1890**, Wales's **seventh** attempt, **1–0** (a single try, no
  conversion), at **Crown Flatt, Dewsbury**, in sleet on a quagmire; scorer **W.J.W. "Buller" Stadden**
  (who was himself by then playing in the north, at Dewsbury — a small irony).
- **Billy Bancroft** (Swansea full-back): 33 consecutive caps 1890–1901 (a longevity record that stood
  to 1954); took all Wales's kicks.
- **Gwyn Nicholls** (Cardiff centre): debut 25 Jan 1896; the "Prince of centres"; only Welshman on the
  1899 Lions tour to Australia; would captain the 1905 win over the All Blacks (Gen 2).
- **Dick Hellings** (forward): 9 caps from 1897.

### 1905: Wales 3–0 New Zealand, Cardiff Arms Park, 16 Dec 1905 — DOCUMENTED
- Wales **3–0**, the **only defeat of the 1905 "Originals"** on their whole tour (they won 34 of 35).
  Crowd **~40,000–47,000** (sources vary — HEDGE, "some forty thousand").
- **Teddy Morgan** try (~23 min), off a rehearsed move worked by scrum-half **Dickie Owen** through
  **Rhys Gabe**. **Gwyn Nicholls** captained.
- **The Bob Deans "no-try":** late on, NZ's Bob Deans claimed to have grounded the ball over the Welsh
  line; Scottish referee **John Dallas** ruled no try. Deans insisted to his death (1908, aged only 21)
  that he scored; even Teddy Morgan reportedly thought he had. NZ grievance persists — render as a
  genuine two-sided dispute, no villain.
- Tactics: Wales countered NZ's weight with a **seven-man (2-3-2) scrum** and pre-rehearsed back moves —
  the four three-quarter game at maturity. (Playing Pasts; Rugby Relics; World Rugby Museum; NZ History.)

### The song — DOCUMENTED (hedge the superlative)
- The crowd/players sang **"Hen Wlad Fy Nhadau" in answer to the haka** — **the first recorded instance
  of a national anthem sung at an international sporting fixture** (frame as "the first recorded
  instance," not a flat "first ever"). Selector **Tom Williams** suggested answering the haka with the
  anthem; **Teddy Morgan** reportedly led the singing; ref Dallas allowed it.
- Origins: words **Evan James**, melody his son **James James**, **Pontypridd, 1856**, first titled
  "Glan Rhondda." (National Library of Wales.)

### Trophies — DOCUMENTED (mind the Grand Slam nuance)
- **Triple Crowns: 1900, 1902, 1905, 1908, 1909, 1911.**
- **Grand Slam nuance:** France entered the championship **officially in 1910**. In **1908** Wales beat
  England, Scotland, Ireland AND France — widely credited as the **first Grand Slam ever**, repeated
  **1909**; but because France was not yet an official member, **1911 is the first *official* Five
  Nations Grand Slam.** State both (first-ever 1908 w/ caveat; first official 1911).

### Players / style — DOCUMENTED
- **Gwyn Nicholls** ("Prince of centres", captain 1905 & the 1902 Crown); **Willie Llewellyn** (wing,
  last survivor of the 1905 XV, d.1973); **Teddy Morgan** (wing, the 1905 try + led the anthem);
  **Rhys Gabe** (centre/wing); **Dickie Owen** (scrum-half, 35 caps — a Welsh record until 1955, tactical
  brain of 1905).
- **Dai "Tarw" Jones** — KEY FIGURE for the from-inside voice: giant forward, a **coal miner from
  Aberaman**; Triple Crowns 1902 & 1905; **suspended in 1907 on a professionalism charge**, went to
  **rugby league**, and scored the **winning try in the 1908 Aberdare league international**. He is the
  collier-player, the pack's "rock of 1905," AND the bleed north, in one man. (WRU tribute.)
- The working-class **Rhondda forwards** — the pit-hardened pack under the golden backs.

### Underneath the gold — the coalfield peak & the bleed — DOCUMENTED
- **South Wales Coalfield peak ~1913:** roughly **233,000 workers** (~a quarter-million; ~one-fifth of
  British miners), ~**56 million tons** a year (~a fifth of British output); **Barry** the world's
  largest coal-exporting port by 1913, Cardiff second. Attribute the coal↔rugby linkage to **Dai Smith
  & Gareth Williams / Gareth Williams, *1905 and All That***.
- **Rugby league arrives in the valleys 1907–08:** Northern Union clubs at **Merthyr Tydfil, Ebbw Vale,
  Aberdare, Barry, Mid-Rhondda, Treherbert.**
- **1 Jan 1908, Aberdare Athletic Ground: Wales 9–8 New Zealand "All Golds"** — recognised as the
  **first rugby league international**; ~20,000 in the freezing cold; Dai Jones the winning try.
- The golden era sat on an industrial base about to peak and then break — the setup for Gen 3.

### Close of the era (1911–1914) — DOCUMENTED
- After the **1911** Grand Slam, Wales declined before the war.
- **WWI: at least 13 Welsh rugby internationals killed** (a 14th died of wounds, 1921). Named:
  **Richard Garnons Williams** (played in the very first Wales international, 1881; killed at **Loos,
  Sept 1915**, aged 59); **Johnny Williams** (prolific wing), **Dick Thomas**, **Horace Thomas**,
  **David Watts** (all Somme, 1916); **Bryn Lewis** (Ypres); **Billy Geen** (killed 31 July 1915, 2nd
  Ypres). Good closing note — the golden generation walking into the war.

### 1. Coalfield collapse 1920s–30s — DOCUMENTED (WJEC; AGOR; NCBI)
- **~440,000 people left Wales 1921–1938, ~85% from the South Wales valleys** (Glamorgan/Monmouth).
  Merthyr Tydfil alone lost ~26,600.
- **241 South Wales mines closed 1921–1936; miners fell from ~270,000 to ~130,000.** Unemployment among
  miners **~42.8% by 1932** (~40% coalfield average, roughly twice the UK rate).
- **1926:** miners locked out (from 30 April), the TUC general strike 3–12 May, a **six-month lockout
  ending in the miners' defeat.**
- Coal↔rugby linkage: attribute to **Smith & Williams, *Fields of Praise*** / **G. Williams, *1905 and
  All That***. This is the **coalfield thesis running in reverse** — the game falls as coal falls.

### 2. The flood north — DOCUMENTED (Welsh Sports Hall of Fame; Rugby Codebreakers)
- **Jim Sullivan** — the emblem: Cardiff debut 1920 aged 16 → **Wigan, June 1921, a record £750 fee**;
  became the greatest full-back of the interwar league game (**922 games, 6,001 points**, 1921–46) and
  never played union for Wales. The best of a generation, gone before he started.
- The interwar years turned the "going north" trickle into a **flood** — driven by mass unemployment and
  union's refusal to pay. Describe as "dozens of internationals" (DROP the Grokipedia 37/27). Robert
  Gate's *Gone North* (148 Welsh union internationals to league 1895–1986) verified in the Gen 1 pass.

  Quentin Smith; reported Nov 2023, found the culture **"unforgiving, even vindictive"**, ~36
  recommendations. Reform: **Abi Tierney** first female WRU CEO (appointed Aug 2023, in post Jan 2024);
  **Richard Collier-Keywood** first independent chair (July 2023).

### 4. Players' strike threat (Feb 2023) — DOCUMENTED (Sky; ESPN)
- Players **nearly boycotted the England match, 25 Feb 2023**, over frozen contracts (~80 expiring June
  2023), the regions' funding crisis, and the **60-cap selection rule**. Deal struck days before; **the
  60-cap rule was cut to 25 caps.**

### 5. On-field collapse — DOCUMENTED
- **RWC 2023:** topped Pool C (beat Fiji 32–26, Portugal 28–8, Australia 40–6, Georgia 43–19); **QF lost
  17–29 to Argentina, Marseille, 14 Oct 2023.** **Alun Wyn Jones** (the world's most-capped Test player,
  158 Wales caps) and **Justin Tipuric** retired May 2023.
- **2024 Six Nations: wooden spoon** (lost all five; opened 0–43 to France in Paris).
- **Record losing streak: 18 consecutive Test defeats — the worst ever by a tier-1 nation.** Last win
  before it: **Georgia, RWC pool, Oct 2023**; the run spanned ~**644 days**, incl. a **first-ever home
  loss to Fiji (Nov 2024)**. World ranking crashed from **No. 1 (2019) to a record-low 14th (2024)**,
  ~12th by 2025.
- **Gatland left again by mutual consent, Feb 2025**, after a **22–15 loss to Italy (8 Feb 2025) — a
  record-extending 14th straight defeat.** **Matt Sherratt** (Cardiff) took interim charge.
- **Streak ended: Wales 31–22 Japan, Kobe, 12 July 2025** (Sherratt interim) — first win in 644 days.

### 6. The turn, the regions, mid-2026 — DOCUMENTED
- **Steve Tandy** appointed permanent HC (announced **21 July 2025**, in post **1 Sept 2025**): ex-Scotland
  defence coach, 2021 Lions, Ospreys, Waratahs — **the first Welsh-born permanent Wales coach since Gareth
  Jenkins (2007).** Possible break from the imported-messiah reflex. First match v Argentina, 9 Nov 2025.
- **2026 Six Nations** (captain **Dewi Lake**, Ospreys): England 48–7 Wales; Wales 12–54 France; Wales
  23–26 Scotland; Ireland 27–17 Wales; **Wales 31–17 Italy (14 Mar 2026) — one win, ending a 3-year Six
  Nations losing run — but a THIRD straight wooden spoon.** World ranking ~**12th** as of mid-2026 (no
  confirmed further move — flag as approximate).
- **Regions crisis:** the four regions near-insolvent; WRU plan to **cut from four to three professional
  teams by 2028–29** (the "PRA25" agreement). **Cardiff went into administration Apr 2025; the WRU bought
  it for £3.8m** (a WRU subsidiary). A proposed Ospreys-linked takeover of Cardiff **collapsed Apr 2026**;
  three licences to be allocated by tender in 2026; **Ospreys & Scarlets seen as most at risk.** The Gen 6
  cull of the clubs now becomes a cull of the regions themselves.
- **UNCERTAIN-RECENT:** the exact end-state of the 4→3 tender and the RWC 2027 outlook are still unfolding
  as of mid-2026 — write them as open/unresolved, not as settled.

### THESIS LANDING (for the finale + chapter close)
- The coalfield is gone (Tower closed 2008); the game no longer has the organic industrial base that once
  produced players and crowds by accident. The "deepest valley" is not just results — it's that Wales must
  now BUILD deliberately what coal once supplied for free, while its union rebuilds from a governance
  collapse and its professional tier shrinks. Close the book's Wales chapter on that, dated mid-2026,
  looking toward RWC 2027 in Australia — honest, unresolved, no false uplift.
