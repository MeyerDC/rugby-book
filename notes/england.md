## GENERATION MAP (redraft) — the story beats

- **Gen 0 — The Boys' Game, and the Story They Told About It (c. 1820s–1870):** folk football; Rugby
  School; the 1845 written rules (the boys write them); Tom Brown's Schooldays spreads it; the clubs
  (Blackheath, Richmond); **1863: Blackheath walks out of the FA over hacking — the FIRST split**;
  and the Webb Ellis myth, told as the manufactured story it is (Bloxam hearsay → 1895–97 Old
  Rugbeian inquiry, certified the year of the Schism).
- **Gen 1 — One Game, Two Englands (1871–1892):** RFU founded Jan 1871; first international v
  Scotland; the game roars north into the Yorkshire & Lancashire mill towns — cup crowds, county
  dominance, working-class champions; the word "amateur" codified (1886) as the South's answer to the
  North's rise. The two Englands are already visible inside one union.
- **Gen 2 — The Great Schism (1893–1895):** the hinge, told twice. **20 Sept 1893, Westminster Palace
  Hotel** — Yorkshire's broken-time motion, defeated with the proxies; the 1895 tightening; **29 Aug
  1895, George Hotel, Huddersfield** — the Northern Union is born. One room, then the other; each
  side's case made from inside.
- **Gen 3 — Two Games Behind One Wall (1895–1945):** parallel histories, braided. North: Challenge
  Cup, 13-a-side (1906), the All Golds & the code going global (1907–08), "going north" (the Welsh
  drain — cross-motif), Rugby Football League (1922), Wembley (1929). South: Twickenham (1909), the
  excommunications (professionalised = tainted for life), France expelled (1931), the golden 1920s.
  Both: the wars — union and league men dying in the same uniform, playing together in wartime, and
  the wall going straight back up in 1945.
- **Gen 4 — The Long Cold War (1946–1994):** league's golden crowds (Odsal 1954 world record);
  union's shamateurism — boot money, the bans on anyone who ever touched league, schoolboys included;
  the crossings ("going north" continues); 1987: the World Cup arrives and the amateur wall starts to
  crack; 1990s shamateurism becomes farce.
- **Gen 5 — Open: The Hundred-Year Debt (1995–2003):** **26–27 Aug 1995, Paris — the game declared
  open**, a century almost to the day after the George Hotel; the chaos of instant professionalism
  (both codes hit by the same money — Super League war in league, club-country war in union);
  Woodward's machine; **Sydney, 22 Nov 2003** — the World Cup won, England's only try in the final
  scored by **Jason Robinson of Wigan**. The braid, holding.
- **Gen 6 — All the Money in the World (2004–2019):** the post-2003 collapse; 2007 & 2019 finals;
  2011 Queenstown; 2015 home-pool exit; Eddie Jones; the Premiership's unsustainable economics
  inflating underneath.
- **Gen 7 — The Bill (2020–2026):** the concussion/CTE reckoning — **both codes sued by their own
  players**; Saracens; Wasps/Worcester/London Irish die; league's parallel crisis; where the two
  games, still separate, stood in mid-2026 — dated snapshot, 2027 outlook.

## GEN 0 BRIEF (1820s–1870 + myth machinery to 1900) — no-wiki; Explore agent, adjudicated (July 2026)

### THE WEBB ELLIS MYTH — the settled machinery
- **Webb Ellis the man**: b. **24 Nov 1806, Salford**; father a cavalry officer killed in the
  Peninsular War (1812); at Rugby **1816–1825 as a local non-fee-paying "foundationer"** (the myth's
  gentleman founder was a scholarship day-boy — use this). Brasenose, Oxford 1825; played the first
  Oxford–Cambridge cricket match at Lord's, June 1827. A contemporary: "an admirable cricketer,
  but… generally regarded as inclined to take **unfair advantages at Football**." Anglican clergyman
  (St Clement Danes 1843–55; Magdalen Laver from 1855). **Died 24 Jan 1872, Menton, France**; never
  married; grave rediscovered 1958. **"Not recorded as having made any comment whatsoever" about
  rugby in his adult life.** (World Rugby Museum, "Who was William Webb Ellis" / "What We Know".)
- **Bloxam letters — the story's sole origin**: Matthew Holbeche Bloxam, old boy & antiquarian,
  **NOT an eyewitness** (left the school before 1823; unnamed second-hand source). First letter to
  *The Meteor* **10 Oct 1876** — four years after Webb Ellis died — crediting "a town boy or
  foundationer of the name of Ellis, Webb Ellis." Second, elaborated letter **22 Dec 1880** pins
  the year 1823 ("on catching the ball, instead of retiring backwards, rushed forwards with the
  ball in his hands"). **DATE FLAG**: hobbyist page puts the second letter at 22 Dec 1876; strong
  sources say 1880 — write "in a second letter four years later" and avoid over-precision.
- **Old Rugbeian Society inquiry**: sub-committee **appointed July 1895** — ONE MONTH before the
  George Hotel meeting (the chapter's central irony; report published **1897**, "The Origin of Rugby
  Football"). It "was unable to procure any first-hand evidence of the occurrence." Witnesses
  UNDERCUT it: **Thomas Harris (left 1828) & John Harris (left 1832)** — handling/running "was
  strictly forbidden" through the 1820s–30s. Thomas Hughes's testimony pointed instead to **Jem
  Mackie**, a famous "running in" player, the practice normalised gradually and **legalised c.
  1841**. The report still concluded Webb Ellis's act was **"in all probability"** the origin.
  Pre-1897 football histories (Vincent, Shearman, Marshall 1885–92) never mention Webb Ellis.
- **Plaque**: bronze tablet at Rugby School unveiled **1900** ("with a fine disregard for the rules
  of football as played in his time…A.D. 1823"). A stray "1895" plaque date in one fetch is
  unverified — use 1900.
- **Named-historian conclusions**: **Tony Collins** — "No one would have been more surprised than
  William Webb Ellis himself" (*The Oval World*); myth elevated in the post-1895 "cultural cold
  war" to claim the game for the gentlemen. **Dunning & Sheard** (*Barbarians, Gentlemen and
  Players*) — first systematic debunking; the Rugbeians' endorsement "was an attempt to assert
  their school's position and authority over a sport that they were losing control of." **William
  J. Baker** (*Albion* 13:2, 1981) — the tale is "a period piece, reflecting more of the era which
  gave it birth than of the event to which it referred"; explicit **Doubleday/baseball parallel**.

### RUGBY SCHOOL & THE 1845 RULES — settled
- Thomas Arnold (head 1828–1841/42) was **largely indifferent to football** — "not an advocate of
  games… as tools for building character"; the Arnold–muscular-Christianity link is a later
  construction via Hughes's novel. (EBSCO Research Starters; Allison.) Do not write Arnold as the
  games-evangelist.
- **28 August 1845**: first written rules of any football code — **37 rules**, drawn up by three
  Sixth Form boys: **William Delafield Arnold (17, son of the late headmaster), W.W. Shirley (16),
  Frederick Hutchins**; approved by the school's sixth-form levée. Content incl. the off-side rule
  verbatim: "No player being off his side shall hack, charge, run in, touch the ball in goal, or
  interrupt a catch." (1845 Laws text, via Wikisource transcription of the primary document.)

### THE BALL — settled (World Rugby Museum; Our Warwickshire)
- **William Gilbert & Richard Lindon**, rival ball-makers to the school by the 1850s; pig's-bladder
  balls, inflated **by mouth** through a clay-pipe stem; shape followed the bladder ("plum-shaped").
- **Rebecca Lindon died 1843 of lung disease linked to blowing green bladders**; Richard Lindon then
  pioneered the **India-rubber bladder** (after the 1851 Great Exhibition) + a brass hand-pump —
  rubber first allowed regulated size/shape. (Use: the artisan's price beneath the boys' game.)
- H-posts/crossbar origin: **UNVERIFIED — treat as legend, omit or hedge.**

### SPREAD & CLUBS — settled/hedged
- ***Tom Brown's School Days*, 1857** (Thomas Hughes, at Rugby 1834–42): bestseller; carried the
  game + "muscular Christianity" worldwide; its match description even reprinted for the 1872
  Yale–Columbia game (US college football lineage).
- Clubs: **Guy's Hospital 1843 — CONTESTED** (circumstantial: an 1883/84 fixture card's "40th
  season"); **Liverpool 1857**; **Blackheath 1858** ("oldest open club in continuous existence" —
  club's own history); **Manchester 1860**; **Richmond 1861**. (Some dates via
  rugbyfootballhistory.com — hobbyist, flag; hedge in prose.)

### THE 1863 FA WALKOUT — settled
- FA formed **Freemasons' Tavern, Great Queen St, London — first meeting 26 Oct 1863**; Blackheath a
  founder member; fight over **hacking + running with the ball** in the draft laws.
- **Francis Maule Campbell** (Blackheath), fifth meeting, verbatim: banning hacking would "do away
  with all the courage and pluck from the game, and I will be bound over to bring over a lot of
  Frenchmen who would beat you with a week's practice."
- **Sixth meeting, 8 Dec 1863**: Campbell withdrew Blackheath — the FA's rules "would destroy the
  game and all interest in it." Other handling clubs stayed out.
- **CLARIFIER (keep)**: 1863 was dribbling-code vs handling-clubs — NOT yet "union vs soccer"; the
  handling clubs had no body until the RFU (1871, 21 clubs).

### FOLK FOOTBALL BACKDROP — settled (brief use)
- Earliest English reference: **William FitzStephen, c. 1174** (London Shrovetide ball games); bans
  from **1314** (London) onward; **Highway Act 1835** banned football on public highways — the state
  killed the commons' game in the same decades the schools codified their own. (Britannica "Folk
  football"; London Museum.)

## GEN 1 BRIEF (1871–1892) — no-wiki; Haiku Explore agent, adjudicated (July 2026)

### RFU FOUNDING — settled
- **26 Jan 1871, Pall Mall Restaurant, 9–10 Haymarket** (NB: named for Pall Mall, located on
  Haymarket — write "the Pall Mall Restaurant off Trafalgar Square" or just name it): **32
  representatives, 21 clubs**. Chair **Edward Carleton Holmes** (Richmond captain); first president
  **Algernon Rutter** (Richmond); secretary/treasurer **Edwin H. Ash** (Richmond), whose **Dec 1870
  letter to the papers** calling for unified rules initiated it. Entrance fee **5 shillings**.
  (blackandblue1871.com; raeburnplacefoundation.org; rugbyfootballhistory — hobbyist, flag.)
- Founding clubs: Addison, Belsize Park, Blackheath, Civil Service, Clapham Rovers, Flamingoes,
  Gipsies, Guy's Hospital, Harlequins, King's College Hospital, Lausanne, Law, Marlborough Nomads,
  Mohicans, Queen's House, Ravenscourt Park, Richmond, St Paul's School, Wellington College, West
  Kent, Wimbledon Hornets — **all southern; hospitals, schools, suburbs** (blackandblue1871).
- **First laws: three ex-Rugby School LAWYERS — Rutter, Holmes, Leonard James Maton**; Maton, laid up
  with a **broken leg**, did most of the drafting; approved 22 June 1871. **Hacking outlawed in the
  1871 laws** (Blackheath had dropped it 1865, Richmond 1866). **Rugby School itself reportedly held
  out (didn't join the RFU until 1890) over hacking** — hobbyist-sourced, hedge with "reportedly."
- **Wasps missed the meeting — LEGEND, two club-lore versions** (wrong venue/day; or right-name
  wrong pub + too drunk to continue). Wasps' own site tells both. Joined by March 1872. Tell AS
  legend.

### FIRST INTERNATIONAL — settled
- Challenge published **8 Dec 1870** in *Bell's Life in London* + *The Scotsman*, signed by captains
  of **five Scottish clubs** (West of Scotland, Edinburgh Academicals, Merchistonians, Glasgow
  Academicals, St Salvator St Andrews): "any team selected from the whole of England," 20-a-side.
- **27 March 1871, Raeburn Place, Edinburgh**: 20-a-side, two 50-min halves; **~4,000 crowd, gate
  over £200** (1s entry). **Scotland won by a goal and a try to a try** (only goals decided matches
  then — a try was just the right to kick). **Angus Buchanan** first-ever international try (England
  disputed it — scrum pushed over; Scottish umpire **Dr H.H. Almond** awarded it); Cross converted;
  England's try: **R.H. Birkett**. England captain **Frederick Stokes** (Blackheath; RFU president
  1874 at 23). (raeburnplacefoundation; scottishrugby.org; WRM.)

### THE RFU vs THE WORLD (1884–1890) — settled
- **1 March 1884, Blackheath, the disputed try**: Kindersley (Eng) scored after **C.W. Berry (Sco)
  knocked the ball BACK**; Scots: any knock illegal; RFU: only forward. Irish umpire **George
  Scriven**: "the ball was knocked back by a Scotchman." Scotland refused the result; **no match
  1885**; Scotland conceded **Feb 1886 "only in the interests of rugby football"** (arbiters
  Macdonald & Marindin). Legacy: the **advantage law**.
- **IRFB founded 1886 by Scotland, Ireland, Wales — WITHOUT England.** RFU refused to join: "as the
  oldest Union, they were the sole guarantees of the laws of the game" + demanded weighted
  representation. **No internationals 1888–89** (isolation). **Joined 1890 with 6 of 12 votes**
  (each founder 2). → posture: owner, not member.

### THE NORTH — settled/hedged
- **Yorkshire Cup, 1877** — first knockout comp in UK rugby; trophy **"t'owd tin pot"**, sterling
  silver, Martin Hall & Co (Sheffield/London), hallmarked 1877; **first final: Halifax beat York**
  at Holbeck, Leeds. Cup ties became mass civic events (specific gate figures PENDING — don't
  invent numbers).
- **County Championship 1889**: Yorkshire champions 1889 unbeaten (aggregate 18 goals 17 tries for,
  1 goal 3 tries against) and 1890; won 5 of the first editions to 1893–94 (hobbyist — flag, hedge).
- **1892: England beat Wales 17–0 with 11 northerners in the XV** (hobbyist — hedge "by one count").
- **DICKY LOCKWOOD** (Richard Evison Lockwood, b. 11 Nov 1867): **woollen printer at a Dewsbury
  mill, 9 shillings a week**; **14 England caps from 1887, England captain Jan–Feb 1894**; twice
  investigated for professionalism, acquitted; **asked RFU permission to play for Heckmondwike when
  not on England duty — refused**, which he called out vs public-school men's freedom; turned
  professional 1895 (Wakefield Trinity captain); **bankrupt 1897**; d. 10 Nov 1915. (Tony Collins
  rugbyreloaded; WRM; ESPN.) ← THE Gen 1→2 bridge character.

### AMATEURISM — settled
- **FA legalises professionalism 20 July 1885** (after northern soccer clubs' pay practices + rival
  "British FA" threat 1884). The RFU watched and chose the opposite path.
- **RFU anti-professionalism regulations 1886**: no payment beyond legitimate expenses; **broken
  time (wage compensation, ~6s/day scale) explicitly banned** (rugbyfootballhistory Schism page —
  hobbyist flag; core fact safe via Collins).
- **Rev. Frank Marshall**: headmaster of **Almondbury Grammar School, Huddersfield**; senior
  Yorkshire rugby figure, referee; amateurism's fiercest enforcer; **1893 helped suspend his own
  town's club, Huddersfield**, for breaches; held rugby "a middle-class pastime"; **2021: Kirklees
  Council refused a blue plaque for him after objections** (ExaminerLive). Bio dates 1845–1906
  partially wiki-tinged — use role, not dates. **Marshall–Miller verbatim exchange: PENDING → Gen 2
  brief.**
- **1888 British tour** (Australia/NZ): privately run by cricket promoters **Shaw, Shrewsbury,
  Lillywhite**; 22 players, captain **Robert Seddon**; 35 rugby matches W27 D6 L2. **WRM: "no doubt
  that the majority of British players were paid to tour," some over £200; players gave statutory
  declarations to the RFU that they were unpaid.** **Jack Clowes (Halifax): took £15 for kit,
  admitted it, offered to repay; declared a professional — the news reached him aboard the
  *Kaikoura* after sailing; forbidden from playing on the whole tour.** The honest man punished;
  the liars capped. (WRM; lionsrugby.com.)

### GAME MECHANICS — settled (WRM scoring pages)
- 15-a-side: Oxbridge pioneered **13 Dec 1875**; first 15-a-side international 1877 (Eng–Ire);
  formalised by RFU 1892.
- Scoring: pre-1886 goals only (from Nov 1875 tries broke ties); **1886: goal 3, try 1**; 1888
  penalty goal (2); 1891–92 try = 2; **1893–94 try = 3, conversion 2**.

## GEN 2 BRIEF (1893–1895) — no-wiki; Haiku Explore agent, adjudicated (July 2026)

- **Motion verbatim: "That players be allowed compensation for bona fide loss of time."** Moved by

  **The famous "~120 proxies in the platform's pocket": UNVERIFIED this pass — do NOT state a

### THE CRACKDOWN 1893–95 — settled
- **1893: Marshall helps suspend HUDDERSFIELD — his own town's club — 8-match ban** over signing
  **George Boak & Jock Forsyth from Cumberland**; Marshall chief witness; contemporary epithet:
  "the man with bell, book and candle facing the evil spirit of professionalism." (ExaminerLive;
  gottfriedfuchs blog — flag.)
- **Collins: the RFU "inverted the normal processes of law"** — accused clubs had to prove
  innocence (Irish Examiner, Collins-derived). Bylaw #1: only all-amateur clubs eligible; HQ and

- Collins (Rugby Reloaded): broken time "was in fact **a battle for position before the inevitable
  showdown over payment for play**."

### 29 AUG 1895, GEORGE HOTEL, HUDDERSFIELD — settled (rugby-league.com; Huddersfield local
history/Daily Chronicle 30 Aug 1895)
- **6:30 pm**; venue chosen roughly equidistant Widnes–Hull. **21 clubs represented**: Batley,
  Bradford, Brighouse Rangers, Broughton Rangers, Dewsbury, Halifax, Huddersfield, Hull, Hunslet,
  Leeds, Leigh, Liversedge, Manningham, Oldham, Rochdale Hornets, St Helens, Tyldesley, Wakefield
  Trinity, Warrington, Widnes, Wigan. **Stockport admitted by wire to the hotel** (telegram/
  telephone — accounts vary) = 22nd.
- **Resolution verbatim: "That the clubs here represented decide to form a Northern Rugby Football
  Union, and pledge themselves to push forward, without delay, its establishment on the principle
  of payment for bona-fide broken-time only."** Carried unanimously.
- **Dewsbury: in the room but held back** — withdrew days later, stayed RFU; local journalist:
  "there wasn't a single supporter who wouldn't say 'Let us have the Northern Union and the sooner
  the better'" (so 20 of the room resigned from the RFU + Stockport). Broken-time rate **six
  shillings a day** (hobbyist/common literature — safe, cross-attested).
- Machinery: 10-man subcommittee for bylaws; committee meetings to alternate Huddersfield/
  Manchester. **First NU season kicked off Sat 7 Sept 1895** (hobbyist — hedge "within nine
  days"); first champions **Manningham** (Bradford) — skip the "4–0" detail, unclear.

### RFU RESPONSE, 19 SEPT 1895, CANNON STREET HOTEL — settled
- New laws 10 days later: union clubs forbidden to play NU clubs; no testimonials; no compensation
  for time/training/travel; **any player playing for OR AGAINST an NU team = professional =
  lifetime ban**; burden of proof on the accused.
- **Enforcement absurdities (Tony Collins, Rugby Reloaded "1895: the aftermath")**: a **Wyke player
  banned for life for merely TRAVELLING with Brighouse Rangers** to a match without playing;
  Elland banned two (one for playing against a team that CONTAINED an NU man); **Jan 1898: Goole
  RUFC ordered not to play a charity match against the "Little Red Riding Hood" pantomime troupe
  because the troupe had earlier played a charity match with Batley.** Beverley FC left with
  virtually no fixtures. **100+ northern players expelled** (hobbyist — hedge "more than a
  hundred").

### THE NUMBERS — settled/hedged
- **RFU clubs: 481 at the 1893 peak → ~244 a decade later** (Oxford Reference; avoid grokipedia).
- **Lancashire + Yorkshire: ~240 union clubs pre-1895 → ~22 by 1897** (Irish Examiner,
  Collins-derived); Lancashire down to 13 by summer 1897 (Northern History journal abstract);
  **Halifax had NO union clubs by 1897; Leeds union "practically non-existent"** (Collins).
- **NU: 22 founding clubs → 59 clubs by first AGM 1896**; Salford & Swinton crossed summer 1896
  (RFU had denied them fixtures — Collins: the union undermined its own loyalists); Barrow (Apr
  1897), Hull KR (June 1897), Leeds Parish Church followed.
- **England: no Home Nations title for 15 years after 1895** (World Rugby Museum, 1890s Part 2).
- **Collins verdicts (verbatim)**: split "driven by fears of working class takeover… rather than
  regionalism" (Routledge blurb of *Rugby's Great Split*); "There was a lot of prejudice involved.
  The RFU didn't like what they saw as the property of the private schools being taken over by
  ordinary people from the north" (Sky Sports interview, 2020); the clubs, "very commercial," were
  the driving force, not the players.

## GEN 5 BRIEF (1995–2003) — no-wiki; Haiku Explore agent, adjudicated (July 2026)

### THE GAME GOES OPEN — PARIS, AUG 1995 — settled
- **26 August 1995, Paris (IRFB meeting, 24–26 Aug): rugby union declared "an open game."** Chairman
  **Vernon Pugh** the architect — verbatim: **"Whether or not we promote it, the game will be openly
  professional within a very short space of time."** (world.rugby.)
- Trigger: the **SANZAR–News Corp deal (~US$550m/10yr, signed July 1995)** for the Southern
  Hemisphere + **Kerry Packer's rival World Rugby Championship** signing up players — the IRB moved
  to keep control (Pugh's logic: participate and control, or lose the game). (world.rugby;
  rugbyworldcup.com.)
- **THE SYMMETRY (chapter's hinge — verify count): 26 Aug 1995 is almost exactly 100 years — 99
  years and 3 days — after the George Hotel (29 Aug 1895).** The union spent a century excommunicating
  men for taking money; then, near the anniversary of the day the North walked out, it legalised
  money itself. Frame as the debt of 1895 called in.
- Mike Catt cited as the first contracted fully professional England player (world.rugby) — minor,
  optional.

### BOTH CODES, SAME MURDOCH MONEY, SAME MOMENT — settled/hedged
- **League's Super League war (1995–96)**: RFL approved a **News Corp deal (~£77–87m)** in **April
  1995**; **proposed club MERGERS (Calder, Cheshire, Humberside etc.) triggered a furious fan revolt
  (announced Good Friday 1995) and were scrapped within weeks.** **Super League launched 29 March
  1996** and moved the game from **winter to SUMMER.** Murdoch (News Corp) vs Packer (Nine/Optus)
  was ALSO the war tearing Australian league apart at the same instant. (superleague.co.uk; ESPN.)
- **USE**: the same media-mogul money hit both English rugbies in the same 12 months — union went
  open, league went summer — a hundred years after one split from the other. The wall's two sides
  were remade by the identical force, together.

### UNION'S CLUB-vs-COUNTRY CIVIL WAR (England, 1995–99) — settled
- New rich owners bought the clubs: **Nigel Wray (Saracens), Sir John Hall (Newcastle Falcons —
  Barcelona-model ambitions), Ashley Levett (Richmond).** RFU vs clubs over who owned/paid/released
  players.
- **"Tour from Hell", June 1998**: clubs refused to release the stars; England sent a depleted squad
  (~20 uncapped) and were battered — **Australia 76–0 (Brisbane, 6 June)**, New Zealand 64–22 and

  season** — both fell out of the top flight; **salary cap introduced 1999** to stop the bleeding.
  The instant free market nearly killed the English club game in four years. (LawInSport.)

### THE WALL REVERSES — UNION BUYS LEAGUE (chapter-critical) — settled

## GEN 7 BRIEF (2020–mid 2026) — no-wiki; Haiku Explore agent, adjudicated (July 2026)

### THE CONCUSSION / CTE LITIGATION — the spine — settled
- **Steve Thompson** (England hooker, **2003 World Cup winner**), diagnosed **early-onset dementia +
  probable CTE in 2020, aged 42**. Verbatim: **"You see us lifting the World Cup and I can see me
  there jumping around. But I can't remember it."** / "I can no longer remember winning the 2003
  Rugby World Cup or even being in Australia." Pledged to donate his brain (Sept 2021). THE cold
  open.
- **Group action** vs **World Rugby, the RFU, the Welsh Rugby Union — AND the Rugby Football League**
  (both codes sued by their own players — the chapter's climax). Lead firm **Rylands Garth**. Other
  named union claimants: Michael Lipman, Alix Popham (Wales), Carl Hayman (ex-All Black, early
  dementia), Ryan Jones (ex-Wales CAPTAIN — dementia), and 2003 winners **Phil Vickery & Mark Regan**
  (revealed Dec 2023).
- **Scale: 1,000+ former players by June 2025** (Washington Post: "more than 1,000"; ~1,100 union +
  league combined per Sports Litigation Alert). Early cohort had ~180+ with early-onset dementia.
  Diagnoses across the group: early-onset dementia, CTE/probable CTE, MND, Parkinson's, epilepsy,
  post-concussion syndrome.
- **Claim**: the bodies "failed to take reasonable action to protect them… despite knowing of the
  risks" of concussive and **sub-concussive** blows.
- **Status at mid-2026: STILL ONGOING, no settlement.** High Court (Justice **Dexter Dias**),
  **23 Dec 2025**, upheld disclosure/case-management orders and cleared the way for "bellwether"
  lead cases; core liability/causation questions unresolved. Governing bodies still contesting.
- **CAUTION (do not overclaim)**: a solicitors'-regulator (SRA) investigation into Rylands Garth
  over an ex-player's (Will Green) pressure allegations exists — DON'T moralise on the lawyers; keep
  focus on players/diagnoses/science.

### THE SCIENCE — settled/hedged
- **Boston University (UNITE brain bank etc.): of 31 former rugby-union players' brains studied,
  ~68% (21) had CTE.** Risk rose with **years of play (~14% per year), independent of diagnosed
  concussions** — i.e. the damage is cumulative sub-concussive load, not just big knocks.
- **Ann McKee (BU neuropathologist), verbatim: "CTE is a preventable disease; there is an urgent
  need to reduce not only the number of head impacts, but the strength of those impacts, in
  rugby."**
- First rugby player confirmed with CTE post-mortem widely reported = **Billy Guyton (ex-Māori All
  Black, NZ, d. 2023, Stage 2 CTE)** — NZ, not British; DO NOT claim a "first British" case
  (unverified).

  CBE 2024; Rob Burrow Centre for MND, Leeds.

  MND 2022). **CAUTION: MND ≠ CTE; the MND Association says the rugby–MND link is UNPROVEN.** Frame
  as the code's grief + a suspected but unestablished association — never state MND was "caused by"
  rugby.
- **RFL sued in parallel**: ~1,100 union+league claimants combined; a league sub-cohort of 168
  tested (ages 26–76, 5 deceased), **64% reporting degenerative-brain-disease symptoms** (Serious
  About RL). Same 23 Dec 2025 ruling covers it. **THE POINT: both sides of the 1895 wall are being
  sued by their own players for the same injury — the one bill that doesn't care about class.**

### THE PREMIERSHIP CLUBS DIE — settled
- **THREE historic clubs gone in ~8 months of 2022–23**: **Worcester Warriors** (HMRC winding-up,
  relegated/administration Oct 2022); **Wasps** (administration Oct 2022; Championship licence
  revoked May 2023 — a founding-era name, dropped to the bottom of the pyramid); **London Irish**
  (went bust June 2023, £30m+ debts, suspended). **Premiership cut from 12 to 10 clubs.**
- Stabilised at 10 through 2024–26. **RFU Council abolished automatic promotion/relegation, 27 Feb
  2026** (criteria-based expansion instead) — **Ealing Trailfinders won the Championship but were
  ruled ineligible to go up.** Newcastle Falcons → **Newcastle Red Bulls (Red Bull takeover, Feb
  2026)**. (Optional colour.)

### THE RFU IN CRISIS — settled (with a golden callback)
- **Record operating loss £37.9m in 2023–24** (vs +£4m the year before); driven by the RWC-year gap
  in home fixtures (revenue down ~£54m).
- **CEO Bill Sweeney total package ~£1.1m for 2023–24 (£742k salary + £358k bonus/LTIP)** — amid
  ~40 redundancies and a poor England year. **141 member clubs forced a Special General Meeting**
  (first in 20 years), **held 27 March 2025, Twickenham**; **motion to remove Sweeney LOST 206–466
  (36 abstentions) — ~66% backed keeping him; he survived.** LTIP scheme halted.
- **Chairman Tom Ilube resigned Dec 2024** over the pay scandal ("a distraction from the game").
- **★ CALLBACK (use to close the institutional arc): Sir Bill Beaumont — the 1980 Grand Slam captain
  the RFU EXILED in 1982 for keeping his own autobiography royalties (Gen 4) — returned as INTERIM
  CHAIRMAN of the RFU in March 2025.** The man amateurism cast out came back to run the professional
  wreck. Institutions, not men.
- **Progressive Rugby** (Haskell, Cudmore + medics) lobbying a 7-point head-injury reform plan
  (blanket 21-day stand-down; cap on contact training; fewer games). The players' pressure group.

### THE MEASURES (reckoning vs resistance) — settled
- **Smart mouthguards mandatory in elite rugby from 1 Jan 2024** (in-game head-impact alerts to an
  independent doctor); **community tackle height lowered to below the sternum from July 2023.** The
  sport is changing the rules and the tech — but at mid-2026 there is **no legal admission of past
  negligence**; the bodies still contest the claims. Reckoning in motion, incomplete.

### SARACENS AFTERMATH — settled (brief)
- Relegated 2020 for the cap breaches; won the Championship; **back in the Premiership 2021–22**;
  one of the stable 10 by 2026. The scandal that closed Gen 6 did not kill the club — it just proved
  the 1895 question (who may pay players, and how) was still the game's live wire.
