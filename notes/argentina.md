**Verified Gen 0 brief (1873–1964):**
- BAFC founded **9 May 1867**, Temple St (now Viamonte); first match 20 Jun 1867 at BA Cricket Club
  ground. Yellow-fever collapse 1870 (~8% of the city dead); refounded **25 Apr 1873** under Hogg.
- **First rugby match: 14 May 1874**, David Mathven's estate, Caballito — after a vote to adopt
  rugby-union laws — Trench XV v Hogg XV. Code churn 1874–76 (reverted to assoc. 1875 over injuries).
  Club's last recorded matches 1881, incl. v **Montevideo CC** (ties to the Uruguay chapter).
- Hogg to *The Standard*: football is the cheapest/best pastime "for the people" — describing
  *football*, not rugby (the fork).
- Exclusion ran both ways: officer **Gallino** feared football could get him discharged from the
  army; locals who played cricket were offended at football invites. Clubs admitted only English
  members through ~1903.
- **First criollo team: 1904**, Centro de Estudiantes de Ingeniería (merger of "La Línea Recta"
  1894 + the students' centre); beat British residents 21–5 in 1905. Football's nationalist revolt
  was earlier: **Argentino de Quilmes 1899** in light-blue-and-white.
- **1890:** both teams + ~2,500 spectators arrested — Juárez Celman's police, paranoid after the
  July 1890 Revolution of the Park, suspected a political meeting in disguise.
- **River Plate Rugby Union, 10 Apr 1899**; founders Buenos Aires FC, Belgrano, Flores, Lomas +
  Rosario AC; RFU-affiliated **until 1932**; first championship won by **Lomas**. Renamed **UAR,
  Nov 1951**. NOT fourth-oldest (see fix above).
- Mechanism: Anglo clubs abandoned football as it professionalised (**CASI 1931**, Lomas 1909,
  Atlético del Rosario 1916 → Plaza Jewell rugby-only, Córdoba AC 1931) and poured grounds+money
  into amateur rugby. SIC schism from CASI **1935** (post-match gathering in Salón Cullen, no women
  present); first CASI–SIC match 9 May 1937, SIC 3–0 → "el Superclásico".
- International record: **91 years, no win over a touring side.** Lions 1910 (lost 3–28; all XV
  Argentine-born) / 1927 (jersey adopted, Gutiérrez proposed light-blue-white) / 1936; Junior
  Springboks 1932 & 1959 (15 from 15); France 1949, 1954, 1960. SA Championship dominance was
  meaningless (geographical isolation; e.g. Brazil 60–0). Campeonato Argentino launched 1940s →
  pushes the game to the Interior (Tucumán). Catholic orders/schools entering by mid-1950s.
