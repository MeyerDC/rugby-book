## GEN 0 — The Sending Church (1826–1923): MISSION HALF VERIFIED

**Source, read in full by hand from the PDF (5 September 2026):** Revd Dr **Finau Pila 'Ahio**,
"Christianity and Taufa'āhau in Tonga: 1800–1850", *Melanesian Journal of Theology* 23-1 (2007),
pp. 22–80 — [PDF](https://biblicalstudies.org.uk/pdf/mjt/23-1_22.pdf), HTTP 200, 59 pages, extracted
and searched locally. **'Ahio is Principal of the Sia'atoutai Theological College in Tonga** — a Tongan
writing Tongan church history from inside, quoting Turner's and Thomas's journals directly. This is
exactly the voice `SUMMARY.md` §2 asks for and it should carry Gen 0.

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE FRAMING CORRECTION — Tonga received before it sent
The Step 0 digest, and my own inherited-threads note above, had Tonga as **the sender** in the region's
mission story. **That is only half true, and the other half is better.**

- Christianity reached Tonga **before the Wesleyans**, carried by **four Tahitians** — **Hape, Tafeta,
  Borabora and Longi**. Hape and Tafeta "**called at Nuku'alofa on their way to Fiji from Tahiti**" and
  decided to stay. The other two joined later.
- **They built a chapel and ran a school**, supported by the chiefs **Aleamotu'a Tupou** and **Ulakai**.
- By the time Turner reached Nuku'alofa (he landed at Hihifo **2 November 1827**), about **300 people**
  were meeting regularly for worship; **nearly 240** attended the service he found in the Tahitians'
  chapel, and he "was impressed by their success."

⭐ **Islanders on their way somewhere else stopped in Tonga, built a school, and taught.** That is the
identical shape as everything the chapter's second half is about — a man from one island maintained by a
community on another, doing work on its behalf. **It is in place in the 1820s, a century before rugby.**

### The Wesleyan arrival
- **1822:** the Wesleyan Methodist Mission's first attempt — pioneer **Walter Lawry** — abandoned after
  **14 months** when his wife's health failed.
- **1826:** "It was not until 1826 that the second Wesleyan Methodist Mission party arrived in Tonga to
  establish their mission in **Hihifo** (the western part of Tongatapu)" — **John Thomas** and **John
  Hutchinson**. The work "began to grow, **in spite of strong resistance from the Tongans**."
- **Early 1828:** Turner and Cross **revived the school the Tahitians had built** — **80 pupils**, and
  **within six months the roll had grown to 150**, several able to "spell out words of five to six"
  letters. The task was to "teach both children and adults" to read their own language.
  ⭐ **The school is the carrying institution from 1828. Everything later — Tupou College, Tonga
  College, the schoolboy rugby that still supplies the national team — sits on top of this.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE COLD OPEN CANDIDATE — the king's body, before rugby existed
**Basil Thomson**, a young civil servant from Fiji who worked in Tonga for ten months, on **Taufa'āhau**:

> "His great natural powers were enhanced by **the most careful athletic training**. As he surpassed his
> fellows in stature and length of limb, so was he their superior in **all sports that demanded skill**.
> **None was so fleet of foot, none could meet him in a wrestling or boxing match**, none could endure
> against him in swimming in the surf, nor handle a *Tafa'anga* (fishing canoe) laden with fish in a sea
> way, as he, none was his match in a fight to the death."

⭐ A contemporary measuring the founder of the Tongan monarchy **by his body and his sport** — decades
before a rugby ball reached the islands. Open the chapter here, or on the baptism below.

### The baptism
- **7 August 1831:** Taufa'āhau baptised, after a feast the day before, at a service attended by
  **more than 2,000 people**. Three of his children were baptised with him.
- The first seven converts at Nuku'alofa had been baptised earlier and given Bible names —
  **Mafile'o, Takanoa, Lavola, Kavamoelelo, Lavemai, Moungaevalu**.
- **Pita Vi**, a native of Ha'apai and one of the first baptised, was sent back to Ha'apai as a teacher
  and is "**regarded as the first Tongan preacher and teacher**." Taufa'āhau, visiting Tongatapu in
  **August 1829**, was "deeply disappointed" that they had sent a Tongan rather than a European.
  ⭐ **The first export is a Tongan, sent by a committee, and the chief's complaint is that he was not
  worth as much as the imported version.** That argument is still running in 2026.

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE ONWARD SENDING — dated, named, and it lands exactly where the book already is
> "Another important result of the revival was **the taking of the gospel to Fiji and Samoa. Tongan
> evangelists and teachers, like Joel Bulu, Sailosi Fa'one, James Havea, and Paula Vea**, and many
> others, went to Fiji with the gospel. **The District Meeting on January 2, 1835, decided to send
> William Cross and David Cargill to Fiji.**"

✅ This **confirms and dates** `14-fiji.md`'s "Wesleyan Methodists established themselves at Lakeba in
1835, **arriving via Tonga**" — and supplies what the Fiji chapter could not: **the meeting that decided
it (2 January 1835) and the names of the Tongans who went.**
⭐ **Tonga was exporting men to do other islands' work, by resolution of a District Meeting, in 1835 —
eighty-nine years before its first rugby Test and a hundred and sixty before the game went open.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ 1. THE SPINE, FROM THE FLOOR OF THE HOUSE OF COMMONS
**Hansard, TONGA BILL [Lords], House of Commons, 11 May 1970** —
[api.parliament.uk](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1970/may/11/tonga-bill-lords),

- What the 1970 Bill did: "**responsibility for external affairs and defence revert to Tonga**", and the

secondary summary.

⚠️ **TRAP — THERE ARE TWO TREATIES, NOT ONE.** Hansard names **1879** (a treaty of friendship,
*voluntarily* concluded, to prevent annexation **by somebody else**) and **1900** (protection). The Step 0
digest gave only 1900. **Do not collapse them.** The 1879 date is the stronger fact for the spine: Tonga
went looking for a protector on its own initiative, twenty-one years before the protectorate.

## GEN 2 — The Protected State (1939–1969): RESEARCH

**Source, downloaded and read by hand:** Amanda L. SullivanLee, **"TAU: A Brief History of the Tongan
Military from the Late Nineteenth Century to the Present"**, MA thesis in History, **University of
Hawai'i at Mānoa, May 2019** —
[PDF](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/bitstream/10125/63196/1/SullivanLee_hawii_0085O_10236.pdf),
HTTP 200, 123 pages, extracted and searched locally. Solomon Islands 102 mentions, Sālote 57, Defence
Force 32, **rugby 1**. ⭐ **This is Tonga's equivalent of the Kanemasu military-migration corpus that
carries `14-fiji.md`'s "muscle trade" argument** — and the author is herself Tongan, writing about
"the Tongan soldiers who enlist" from an explicitly stated position outside the kingdom.

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE LINE THAT ANSWERS THE CHAPTER'S OWN QUESTION
> "**Although Tonga was never formally colonized in the way many island nations continue to be, the
> exploitation and fetishization of Tongan male bodies in the interest of warfare is no less present.**"

SullivanLee returns to the formulation three times — "its particular articulations in **a nation that was
never formally colonized**". **Never annexed, and exploited anyway.** That is the answer to the question
Gen 0 sets up: if there is no coloniser to blame, what explains the export? An academic source, written
by a Tongan, says the absence of formal colonisation did not prevent it. Transfers directly to rugby.

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE 1941 ASSESSMENT — AND IT IS THE SAME SENTENCE THE RUGBY PRESS STILL WRITES
**Lieutenant-Colonel John McLeod, M.C.**, early 1941, on where to site Allied bases (quoted in Priday,
*The War from Coconut Square*, p. 27):
> "Found the Tongans keen to make good soldiers, and they **took to drill and manoeuvres like ducks to
> water**. New Zealanders have a flair for getting the best of Polynesian races, for they know that, as
> with their own Maoris, **the blood of warriors and gentlemen flows in their native veins**."

SullivanLee's reading: "Tongans, as well as other Polynesians were touted as being desirable because they
were **strong and brave but also submissive**. Instead of seeing indigenous soldiers as equal partners,
white officers viewed them more like **one of the many weapons in the military's advancing arsenals** —
Polynesians were **dangerous and desirable but needing of white direction in order to be useful**."

⭐⭐⭐ **THREAD THIS HARD.** It is the same compliment, word for word in structure, as the rugby writing
this book has already quoted: "**the big, sturdy men of Apia**" (1924, in `15-samoa.md` Gen 1, which notes
people were still writing that sentence a hundred and two years later) and Stephen Jones after Nantes in
`14-fiji.md` Gen 6 — "Give them space and time and they move the ball well." **Physically gifted, needing
somebody else's structure.** A 1941 colonel and a 2007 fly half made the same assessment of the same
bodies. That is Section 5's argument arriving from outside sport entirely.

### VERIFIED WAR FACTS
- **3 September 1939: Tonga declared war on Germany.** Queen **Sālote** called on her people to volunteer
  and initiated the creation of the **Tongan Defence Force (TDF)**.
- The Queen's consort and Tonga's Premier, **Prince Tungī Mailefihi**, was made **colonel-in-chief of the
  TDF** — and **died suddenly in 1941**, leaving her to face the war alone.
- **Two platoons — fifty TDF soldiers — left Tonga** and fought alongside Fijians, Americans and New
  Zealanders at **Guadalcanal, New Georgia and Bougainville**.
- They served in the **First Commando Fiji Guerillas**, "comprised of skilled Fijian, Solomon Islander,
  Tongan, and New Zealander soldiers."
  ⭐ **Tongans and Fijians served in the same unit.** `14-fiji.md` Gen 2 has 8,000 Fijians in the Solomons
  "where they gained a reputation for bravery." **The two chapters' war generations are the same campaign.**
- First TDF group led by **Lieutenant Henry Taliai** with New Zealander **Lieutenant Benjamin Masefield**;
  they **landed on Guadalcanal on 19 April 1943**, tasked with combing the island for remaining Japanese.
- **Malaria, typhus and dengue** were "incredibly pervasive and debilitating."
- **The Battle of Munda Point** produced the Tongan platoon's casualties.
- ⭐ **A Tongan period voice.** The **Speaker of the Tongan Assembly, Nuku**, on those dead: "**Theirs was
  the blood which Tonga shed in the blood poured out by the Allied Nations for Right and Freedom.**"

### ⚠️ FIGURE REJECTED — inflated by repetition
Search summaries state "New Zealand helped train approximately **2,000 Tongan troops who saw action in
the Solomon Islands**." The thesis, which is specific and sourced, says **two platoons, fifty men** went
to the Solomons. The 2,000 figure is at best total TDF enlistment, not overseas service. **Use fifty. Do
not print 2,000 for the Solomons.** (This is exactly the class of error the France proofread caught — a
statistic inflated by repetition.)

## GEN 3 — Ballymore (1970–1986): RESEARCH — the richest verified material in the chapter

### Independence, verified
**US Department of State, Office of the Historian** — [history.state.gov/countries/tonga](https://history.state.gov/countries/tonga):
Tonga became fully independent on **4 June 1970**, when it "officially withdrew from its protectorship
agreement with Great Britain." The 1900 agreement was titled the "**Treaty of Friendship and
Protectorship**" and "delegated sole authority of Tongan foreign relations to the British Foreign Office,
**although it maintained Tongan autonomy**."
⚠️ The State Department says "protectorate"; Hansard's Braine says "never been a British colonial
dependency." Both are true and not in conflict — but **prefer Hansard's wording and the treaty's own
title** in prose, and avoid the bare word "colony" in either direction.

### ⭐⭐⭐ 1973 — AND IT WAS A TOUR, NOT A ONE-OFF. AUSTRALIA WON THE FIRST TEST.
Two Rugby Australia retrospectives, both opened and read in full:
[50 years on](https://www.rugby.com.au/news/50-years-on-tongas-gift-to-australia-in-the-famous-upset-of-1973-202376)
and ["Made us look hopeless"](https://www.rugby.com.au/news/made-us-look-hopeless-classics-reflect-on-brutal-1973-tonga-test-2023713) (13 July 2023).

**Correction to the Step 0 digest and to this file's earlier note:** the 16–11 was the **second Test of a
two-Test tour**. **Geoff Richardson** (fly half that day): "**We won the first Test in Sydney and NSW

the second Test." **Do not write it as a standalone giant-killing** — Tonga lost the first Test and a
midweek match, then won at Ballymore.

- **30 June 1973, Ballymore, Brisbane: Tonga 16, Australia 11** — **four tries to two** (the try count is
  the Tonga Rugby Union's own page).
- Captain and No. 8 **Sione Mafi**, who scored inside two minutes. Fullback **Valita Ma'ake**, the

- Richardson: "I remember their scrum being very strong – they used the ball at the back well and **Sione
  Mafi, who I rated as a world class Number 8**, was brilliant on the day. **At the end, they'd scored
  more points and that usually wins you the game.**"
- **John Cole** (wing) "recalls being bundled over the sideline on more than one occasion as the Tongans
  brought **unmatched physicality** to Ballymore."
- **Mark Loane**, 18 that day, on Mafi: "he still remembered Sione Mafi running over the top of him and
  smiling … **a smile he'd wake up having nightmares about**."

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE KEFU ARC — THIS FAMILY IS THE WHOLE OF SECTION 5 IN ONE HOUSEHOLD
