## Russia's expulsion from the 2003 qualifiers (second source for the scout's unverifiable page)

- **fact:** The Irish Examiner reported that Russia had been expelled from the Rugby World Cup qualifying tournament after it was found to have fielded three ineligible players. The Spanish Rugby Union had made the complaint, about three men born in South Africa, after its two-leg defeat by Russia in October and November 2002. An independent judicial officer found that Russian officials could not provide evidence that the three were eligible. Russia had been due to play Tunisia on 25 January (2003) for the right to meet the United States for a World Cup place; that fixture was cancelled. Russia was also fined and had two weeks to appeal.
  - url: https://www.irishexaminer.com/sport/rugby/arid-30085340.html
  - quote: "Russia have been expelled from the Rugby World Cup qualifying tournament after it was found they fielded three ineligible players."
  - quote: "The Spanish Rugby Union made a complaint relating to the trio"
  - quote: "who were all born in South Africa"
  - quote: "An independent judicial officer found that Rugby Union of Russia (RUR) officials were unable to provide evidence that the three were eligible to play"
  - quote: "with the winner going on to play the United States for a place at the World Cup in October"
  - status: VERIFIED
  - note: with the federation's ledger this completes the 2003 route: Spain lost to Russia 3–36 in Madrid (27 Oct 2002) and won 38–22 at Krasnodar (24 Nov 2002), so lost on aggregate; took Russia's place; beat Tunisia 33–16 at Valence d'Agen (15 March 2003); lost to the United States 13–62 in Madrid (12 April) and 13–58 in Florida (27 April). The ledger lists one Tunisia match, not two legs. ESPN carries the same story ('Russia expelled from RWC'03') but its page is a script shell the checker cannot read. **The weight of this for the next generation is obvious (Spain itself is sanctioned over eligibility in 2018 and 2022); the prose may note the echo, dated, without predicting.**

## The Zorrilla final, 17 April 2016: El Español read in full

- **fact:** El Español's report opens: 'It was not just any day in Valladolid… The whole city dressed up to host the great final of the King's Cup, played by the local teams El Salvador and VRAC Quesos Entrepinares.' ## Adjudication of the scout dossier (main thread)

- **2003 route:** now double-sourced (above). The Poland score: the ledger has 27–15, the French page 22–15; the ledger stands.
- **Romania v Spain, 14 October 2006:** the scout could not source the score; the federation's ledger has it (España Rumanía 20-43, Madrid, Clasificación Cpto. Mundo 2007).
- **Who scored the winning try at Monaco:** La Nueva says Ignacio Martín "in the last second"; Rugbydump says "finished by Goenaga in the corner". **Conflict: do not name the scorer.** The score, 22–19, and the last-play try are agreed.
- **Zorrilla crowd:** 25,000 to 26,000 across the reports of the day and the federation; 26,500 only in a 2025 article. Print "some 26,000, by the federation's count" or "between 25,000 and 26,000".
- **González Cancho:** elected 14 July 2012, resigned 14 April 2014 (the yearbook's presidents list gives only "2012" and "2014"). The 2021 plea is reported by El Día de Valladolid; attribute and date it.
- **"2012 did not count towards 2015"** is an inference from World Rugby's description of a combined 2013–14 table. Say what the table was; do not say what did not count.
- **French-based players:** only squad lists. The prose may say that the federation's own 2012 squad sheet grouped players under their federations and that one heading was the French federation's. It may not say who was French-born.
- **Superibérica 2009:** one blog. Leave out or keep to a clause.
- **El Salvador's founding, 1960 or 1961:** Generation 2 used the Diario de Valladolid's 1961 for the priest's request. Unchanged.
