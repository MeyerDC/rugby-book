# Spain, Generation 4 — El País read in full, by hand

Main thread, 10 October 2026. Adds to `gen4-scout.md` the passages of each report that the scout did not quote. No chapter prose.

- **fact:** Galashiels, 2 October 1999 (El País, 3 October, headline 'España pierde su única oportunidad'): the report says the match was the one chance Spain had 'at a middling level', South Africa and Scotland being 'mission impossible'. Kovalenco 'kicked five penalties out of six'. Spain did not run the ball until it was losing 17–12; it tried once, a mistake by Kovalenco ended the move, and he kicked at goal again: 17–15. Uruguay, who had 'territorial dominance above 70%' at times and won three times as many scrums, then scored two more tries from interceptions.
  - url: https://elpais.com/diario/1999/10/03/deportes/938901613_850215.html
  - quote: "Frente a Suráfrica (el día 10) y Escocia (el 16) será ya misión imposible"
  - quote: "que pasó cinco golpes de seis"
  - quote: "sin atreverse a jugar a la mano salvo al final cuando ya perdía por 17-12"
  - quote: "Uruguay, que llegó a tener dominios territoriales superiores al 70%"
  - quote: "triplicó también en melées ganadas a España"
  - status: VERIFIED
  - note: this settles two things: Spain scored five penalties (15 points), not six; and the report itself dates the Scotland match to the 16th. Its tone ('humillaciones', 'un modesto rival') is the reporter's and is to be attributed, not adopted.

- **fact:** Murrayfield, 2 December 1998 (El País, 3 December, 'España gana a Portugal y se clasifica para la Copa del Mundo'): Portugal scored two converted tries and a penalty; Scotland had already beaten Portugal 88–15, and Spain would play Scotland on the Saturday 'with nothing at stake'. The report doubted the value of the place: Spain 'will hardly be able to … avoid scandalous defeats', so qualification 'does not look like a clear promotion of this sport'.

- **fact:** The column of 7 December 1998, 'Un comparsa en la Copa del Mundo' (a bit-part player at the World Cup): 'Is such humiliation worth it once more?' It records that to qualify Spain 'only had to beat an inferior rival, to whom it had lost only once in a history of 19 matches', and that a Spanish selection had been playing French clubs in the second division of the European Cup that season, conceding almost 300 points in seven matches.
  - url: https://elpais.com/diario/1998/12/07/deportes/912985222_850215.html

  - quote: "Es muy fácil echarle la culpa a que se tiene un presupuesto bajo en comparación con Inglaterra, Francia y los grandes"
  - status: VERIFIED
  - note: one columnist's verdict in the week of qualification; the harshest contemporary voice found. Set beside the federation's own later line that 1999 was the peak.

- **fact:** Murrayfield, 10 October 1999 (El País, 11 October, 'España cae ante Suráfrica'): Spain went out 'to delay as long as possible the inexorable rain of tries'. South Africa fielded reserves; the report says three black players started, among them the wing Breyton Paulse, and calls that a first for a side that had been all-white under apartheid. Spain was 'solid in the tackle'; it held out for 31 minutes until Paulse broke the line and the captain André Vos scored; Francisco Puertas was left dazed on the grass after a tackle by Otto; Malo went off injured; five of South Africa's seven tries came through the scrum. 47–3.
  - url: https://elpais.com/diario/1999/10/11/deportes/939592827_850215.html

  - quote: "Hasta que Paulse rompió la defensa y el capitán Andre Vos convirtió el primer ensayo"

  - note: the claim about three black players in a Springbok starting side is El País's; the South Africa chapter is the place to check it before repeating it as fact. Attribute.

## Cross-chapter note (not a source)

The Uruguay chapter opens its Generation 4 on this same match, from the other side: "October 1999. At Netherdale, in Galashiels, in the Scottish Borders, a forty-year-old Uruguayan number eight picked the ball up from the base of a scrum and drove over the line", Diego Ormaechea, Uruguay's first World Cup try, and "beating Spain 27–15". The Spain chapter must agree with it (Netherdale; Ormaechea's try from a scrum; 27–15) and may point to it.
