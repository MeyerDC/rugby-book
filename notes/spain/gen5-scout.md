# Spain, Generation 5 scout: "The Long Slump" (2000 - early 2018)

Scope: generation-wide scouting pass on the four missed World Cups (2003, 2007, 2011, 2015), the coaches and the French-based turn, the Zorrilla final of 17 April 2016, Valladolid, the federation's money, Olympus XV and the Superiberica, sevens and Rio 2016, women's fifteens, and the run-in to 2018. Dates, scores and venues already in the yearbook extract are repeated only where a source adds colour or the route to qualification. Best new material: the federation's own weekly bulletins (2006, 2010, 2012; text PDFs), the July 2014 General Assembly minutes (the money), and the 2016 match reports. Bulletins before about 2005 are image scans and could not be used.

## Conflicts

- **Crowd at Zorrilla:** "more than 25,000" (COPE), "25.000" (El Espanol), "26.000" (OKDiario, FER "23 momentos"), "26.500" (Tribuna de Valladolid, 2025, as a record), "aforo para más de 26.000" (the referees' committee, before the match; a capacity, not a count). The FER's "26.000" is the round figure; 26,500 appears only in a 2025 article.
- **When a king last attended rugby:** El Espanol says 1905 (Alfonso XIII at Spain v Italy at Montjuic); OKDiario says 87 years earlier (which would be 1929) at the same match. The book should not state a year without a third source.
- **Romania 19 March 2011:** the yearbook lists Romania 64, Spain 8; the federation's March 2012 bulletin lists the Bucharest fixture as 43-5. The 2010-11 yearbook line-up page says 64-8.
- **Romania 27 March 2010:** the text of the 29 March 2010 bulletin says 48-3; the table in the same bulletin shows "43 3". The yearbook says 48-3.
- **Monaco quarter-final:** Rugbydump has "Ireland ... lost the 1st quarterfinal to Spain (7-12)"; La Nueva has "superar a Irlanda por 12-7". Both agree Spain won. The final, 22-19, is consistent across sources.
- **Founding year of El Salvador:** the club's page (via search summary) gives 1960; the brief gives 1961. Not resolved (site blocked).
- **VRAC "first Spanish side in Europe":** VRAC's page says it was the first Spanish team to play a European international competition (European Shield 2001-02); the El Salvador summary says El Salvador was the first club to win a round of the Shield (2004-05). These are different claims, not a contradiction; keep them apart.
- **Russia in the 2003 repechage:** the French page says Spain advanced "after the disqualification of Russia (players not selectable)" while also showing Spain winning 38-22 at Krasnodar after losing 36-3 at home (aggregate 41-39 to Russia). That Russia was disqualified is the encyclopedia's account; I did not find a second source.
- **Special note:** the brief says Spain's 2007 failure came in the "Georgia/Romania/Portugal" round; the federation's bulletins show the route was different: Czech Republic (round 4), then a group with Romania and Georgia (round 5); Portugal was a separate route and reached the repechage via Georgia.
