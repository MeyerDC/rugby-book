## GENERATION MAP (Step 0, 23 September 2026) — ✋ APPROVED at Gate 0, 23 September 2026

**The carrier test, applied.** The federation's own records show the arrangement changing three ways at
once, and the map follows them:
- **Where the game was run from** (FER presidents, Anuario 2021-22, Parte 0): Barcelona 1923 → Madrid
  from the 1927 merger (Barcelona and Madrid alternating to 1933) → Madrid only, 1933–2001 → the regions
  from 2001 (Pontevedra, Valladolid, San Sebastián, Valladolid).
- **Who won the championship** (Anuario, Parte IV): Catalan clubs 1923–35 → Barcelona clubs against the
  Falange's student union (S.E.U. de Madrid) in the 1940s–50s → Madrid's university colleges (Cisneros,
  CAU, Arquitectura) 1967–88 → Getxo, Sevilla and Valladolid from 1990.
- **What the championship was called**, which is the regime's own label on each era: *Campeonato de
  España* (1923–35) → ***Campeonato de S.E. el Generalísimo*** (1940–76) → ***Copa de S.M. el Rey***
  (from 1976–77).

**And the game came from France, not Britain.** Aleu learned it at Toulouse; the first visiting sides
were from Toulouse and the Basque-French coast; FIRA, France's answer to the International Board, was
Spain's international home. **This settles the Section 6 naming question** held open since ch. 17 — see
the note at the end of this map.

**Generation question:** *How did a French university game become Catalan in two years, and why did
Madrid then take it?* Subplots 1–3 answer the first half, subplot 4 the second. No World Cups in this
generation. "What this generation leaves behind" is written at G2 and hands over the 1927 merger.

**1. "Too Rough and Brutal" (1894–1920): the game that did not take.**
- *Answers:* why rugby failed to root before 1921, while football became a mass sport. **The
  footballers knew rugby and chose football anyway.**
- *Scene (the generation's cold open):* **Bonanova velodrome, Barcelona, February 1901**. FC Barcelona and
  Català FC players try "*algunos ensayos de foot-ball Rugby*". A 1902 profile of FC Barcelona's
  first teams lists the rugby behind them: **Joan Gamper** (the club's founder) at L'Union Athlétique in
  Lyon, **Arthur Witty** at school in Liverpool, **Alfonso Albéniz** at Racing Club de Paris.
- *Also:* the 1894 gymnastics treatise (García Fraguas) describing foot-ball rugby; the Mercè festival
  match planned for 1910 "*por primera vez en España*"; and **Monelos park, A Coruña, 12–13 February 1911**: officers of the ships *Liverpool*
  and *Gloucester* ("buques"; whether they were Royal Navy cruisers is to verify) play rugby for the town after their football matches; the verdict: "*demasiado fuerte y
  brutal*".
- *Institutions and people:* British naval crews; the Barcelona sports press (Toulouse sides expected at
  the Hipódromo in 1903; a club of Catalans and foreigners planned in 1904); Dr **Philippe Tissié**'s
  school-rugby lectures in Madrid; the professor **Marcelo Sanz** warning against football.
- *Voice sought:* the 1911 Coruña and Barcelona press (Torrebadella quotes both); Bonet (1911).
- *Handoff:* the game was known but had no carrier, until a Catalan came back from Toulouse.

**2. The Veterinarian from Toulouse (1921–1922).**
- *Answers:* who carried it in, and why France.
- *Scene:* **Sant Boi de Llobregat, 4 July 1921**, the founding of the Unió Esportiva Santboiana; or
  **2 May 1922** at the old Barcelona hippodrome, Santboiana 3–0 CN Barcelona, a try by Dubois.
- *Institutions and people:* **Baldiri Aleu Torras (1894–1975)**, a veterinary student at Toulouse,
  where Stade Toulousain grew out of student groups; Sant Boi's civic associations; the Stade
  Toulousain model.
- *Voice sought:* **Aleu's manifesto "Entorn el Futbol-Rugby"**, *La Veu de Catalunya*, 1922. Find the
  text (ARCA or the Biblioteca de Catalunya press archive).
- *Handoff:* one club makes a game; the next subplot is the city taking it up.

**3. Nineteen Teams (1922–1923): Barcelona's boom.**
- *Answers:* why Barcelona took it up so fast, and whose game it was.
- *Scene:* **the hippodrome at Can Tunis, 20–21 May 1923**, when Toulouse Lalande Olympique play
  Santboiana and then a Catalan selection (9–0 to the French, who noted the Catalans' progress).
- *Institutions and people:* the **Copa Real Sociedad de Carreras** (April 1922); the **Catalan
  federation** (statutes September 1922, Aleu president); the **FER** (13 April 1922, or 1923, to
  resolve); the **first Campeonato de España, January 1923**; the clubs, among them the **CADCI**, the
  autonomist shop-workers' union, and university faculty sides (Medicine, Pharmacy). *Class:*
  Torrebadella's reading is that it was the game of the **petite bourgeoisie and the liberal professions**
  in a city where football was turning working-class and professional. *And Catalanism:* the 1918–19
  autonomy campaign, and Acció Catalana and Estat Català in 1922.
- *Voice sought:* *Stadium* (1 February 1923); the French report of May 1923 (Delsol).
- *Handoff:* the game has a federation called *Spanish*, and it is entirely in Barcelona.

**4. The King at the Metropolitano (1923–1927): Madrid wants its own.**
- *Answers:* how the game reached Madrid, and why the federation ended up there.
- *Scene:* **Stadium Metropolitano, Madrid, 20 May 1923**, the same weekend as Can Tunis. A promotional
  match on the newly opened ground's grass, before about 10,000, with **Alfonso XIII** watching (he had
  seen the game in London and the Basque Country).
- *Institutions and people:* the **army's Escuela Central de Gimnasia, Toledo (1919–36)**, whose
  infantry academy side reached the **1926 national final**; Real Madrid; the **Federación Centro
  (1925)**; the breakaway **Rugby Unión Española (1927)** and its unsanctioned match with France (FER
  ledger: **1 March 1927, Madrid, 6–66**); the **merger at the end of 1927**, with the seat moving to
  Madrid. Also the Basque-French exhibitions (Tolosa, 24 June 1923; Bilbao, 25 May 1924), a coda
  showing the French coast as a second route in.
- *Voice sought:* *La Libertad* and *Madrid-Sport* (1923), in Torrebadella; the 1927 match reports.
- *Handoff to Gen 1:* a federation split between the city that made the game and the capital that now
  ran it.

**Held for the author at Gate 1:** Las Leonas, and whether the women's side gets subplots of its own in
Gens 4–6. The default is passages within those generations, not a separate subplot.

## GEN 1 SUBPLOT PLAN (G1, 24 September 2026) — ✋ APPROVED at Gate 1, 24 September 2026

Planned in the main thread with no searching, from the map, the conflicts below, Gen 0's closing
section and `notes/italy.md`. Anything marked *(recalled)* is memory, not evidence. It is a lead for S1
and is not to be built on until verified.

**Generation question:** *Who owned Spanish rugby: Barcelona or Madrid?* **The plan's answer, to be
tested by the research:** the answer split three ways. **Madrid took the office.** The presidency
alternated until 1933 and was Madrid's alone from then on. **Barcelona kept the field**: Catalan clubs
won every championship from 1923 to 1935. **The national side and the international seat were fought
over between them.** Subplot 1 is the settlement at work, 2 is Barcelona's claim, 3 is the verdict at
home and abroad. No World Cups in this generation. "What this generation leaves behind" is
written at G2 and hands over **July 1936**.

**Picked up from Gen 0's close:** the January 1928 settlement; **Gutiérrez** (Madrid) president in
1928; **Aleu** (Barcelona) back in the chair in 1929; "the two cities would pass the federation back and
forth". **Gen 0 already tells the first match against France**: the Metropolitano, **26 May 1927**,
France 66–6, unsanctioned by the FER, picked by the Centro federation alone with **one Catalan** in the
side, and called "the first official match" by the Madrid press. Gen 1 recalls it in one clause where it
does work (subplot 1's comparison) and never retells it. *(G1 first draft said "1 March 1927", the
ledger's date. Gen 0 set that aside at Gate 2 for Torrebadella's 26 May; corrected on the author's
question, 24 Sep 2026.)*

**1. The Settlement (1928): the merged federation takes the field.**
- *Answers:* what the January 1928 merger produced in practice: **the rematch with France, this time
  sanctioned**, and a side picked by a merged federation with a Madrid president. **Whose players were
  in it**, counted club by club, is the first piece of evidence for the generation's question, and 1927
  gives the baseline: one Catalan in a Madrid-picked side.
- *Scene:* **Bordeaux, 1928**, the first match the FER itself calls official. It came after the Madrid
  press had already given that title to the 1927 match, so **two "first official" Tests, a year apart,
  one for each side of the quarrel**.
  ⚠️ **Date to resolve at S1:** **8 March 1928** (Anuario ledger) vs **19 April 1928** (FER history,
  "primer partido oficial"). **Both dates were Thursdays**, so find the match report itself (French press,
  Spanish press) rather than choosing between the two federation sources. One may be the date the match
  was agreed or announced. Also check the ledger for a second 1928 match, in case the two dates are two
  fixtures. **Weigh the ledger with care:** it gave the 1927 match as 1 March, and Gen 0's Gate 2
  preferred 26 May, so its dates for these years have been wrong once already.
- *Institutions and people:* the merged **FER** and its statutes (Torrebadella 2024 covers the merger);
  **Enrique Gutiérrez** (who had led the 1927 breakaway); the selectors; the **FFR** as the opponent.
  The score, set against 66–6, measures what a year of settlement had done.
- *Voice sought:* the French match report (*L'Auto*, the Bordeaux press); the Madrid and Barcelona
  reaction to the selection. Did Barcelona complain about Madrid picking the side?
- *Handoff:* Spain's only home Test so far had been Madrid's, in 1927, and had been a rout. In 1929 the
  presidency goes back to Barcelona, and Barcelona gets a Test of its own.

**2. The Exposition Match (1929–1930): Barcelona stages the national side.** *(The generation's cold
open.)*
- *Answers:* how Barcelona made its claim in public. Madrid had staged the first home Test, in 1927,
  without the federation's sanction, and lost 66–6. **Barcelona staged the first home Test the whole
  federation stood behind, and the first win**, in the year of Aleu's return, during the
  **International Exposition**. *(Check at S1 whether the ledger has any other home Test between May
  1927 and May 1929.)*
- *Scene:* **Barcelona, May 1929: Spain 9–0 Italy**, Italy's first Test. ⚠️ **Date to resolve at S1:**
  **19 May** (Anuario) vs **20 May** (FIR, both its timeline and its head-to-head page; one body, so this
  is **one source against one**). The dates are **Whit Sunday and Whit Monday**, both holidays in
  Catalonia, so the weekday settles nothing. The Barcelona press of the day decides it. Under the
  source-conflicts rule, if nothing decides it the prose gives both. **Venue to find.** *(Recalled:* the
  Exposition's Montjuïc stadium opened that same week. *Unverified. Do not assume the match was
  played there.)*
- *Also:* the return match, **Milan, 29 May 1930, Italy 3–0 Spain**: Italy's first win (FIR,
  already verified in `notes/italy.md`). These are the first two of the 27 meetings, of which Spain won
  three.
- *Institutions and people:* **Aleu's** FER; the Exposition's organisers; the **FIR** (Vaccaro), a
  federation barely eight months old; the scorers and the Spanish XV (club by club again).
- *Voice sought:* Barcelona's sports press on the day; the Italian press (*La Gazzetta dello Sport*;
  *Lo sport fascista*, already cited in the Italy notes for December 1928).
- *Handoff:* the national side's best day was Barcelona's, and the federation still had to settle
  where it lived.

**3. Who Spoke for Spain (1931–1936): the verdict at home and abroad.** *(Subplots 3 and 4 of the
first draft, merged at Gate 1 by the author's decision, 24 Sep 2026.)*
- *Answers:* the rest of the question, in two parts. **At home:** how Madrid kept the presidency for
  good from 1933 while Catalan clubs kept winning the championship, under the **Second Republic**
  (April 1931) and the **Catalan Statute** (1932). **Abroad:** who represented Spanish rugby in Europe.
  The index records that the **Catalan federation was admitted to FIRA and sponsored the FER**. If
  that holds, Barcelona held the international seat while Madrid held the national office, and that is
  the generation's sharpest answer. It is also why the home half belongs in the same subplot: the
  office, the field and the seat are one picture.
- *Scene:* **28 March 1936, the last Test before the war** (a Saturday; opponent and venue from the
  Anuario ledger, folder 1045). Alternatively, the FIRA meeting that admitted the Catalans. The home
  half needs no scene of its own. If S1 turns up a Campeonato final between a Catalan and a Madrid club
  in 1933–35, it can serve as an inner scene.
- *Institutions and people:*
  - **At home:** the **FER presidency** (Anuario folder 1771: who, from where, each year); the
    **Federació Catalana de Rugby**; Madrid's sides, among them the army school at **Toledo** (to 1936)
    and the university clubs; **the Basque clubs**, which reached rugby only in the 1930s (per
    Torrebadella). They get a passage: a third region entering a two-city argument. The class and
    Catalanism thread from Gen 0 (CADCI, the liberal professions) continues **if** the sources carry
    it into the 1930s. It is not to be asserted from Gen 0 alone.
  - **Abroad:** **FIRA**, founded at **Turin in 1933** by Italy, France, Germany, Romania and
    Czechoslovakia (FIR timeline). ⚠️ Other sources give **1934**. The Italy notes prefer the FIR, and
